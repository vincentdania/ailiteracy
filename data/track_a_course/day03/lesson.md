# The Documents of Your Job

**Read time: 12–15 minutes · Task: 10 minutes**

Every job has three to five documents it produces again and again. Not exotic documents — the ones you have written dozens of times and will write dozens more. Today you make AI produce yours, in the correct format, in minutes.

## Name your repeating documents

- **Civil servants:** memos and official correspondence in civil-service format, minutes, submissions.
- **Teachers:** lesson plans, schemes of work, exam questions and marking guides.
- **SME owners:** price lists, customer agreements, quotes and receipts.
- **NGO staff:** concept-note sections, activity reports, field-visit summaries.
- **Bankers and admin staff:** formal emails, internal memos, briefing notes.
- **Jobseekers:** CV updates and cover letters.

Pick the one you write most often. That is today's target.

## The mistake to avoid

Do not ask AI to "write a memo". It will produce a memo from the internet — American in structure, wrong headings, wrong tone. Your office has its own way: its own heading block, its own routing line, its own phrases.

**The move that fixes this: paste one old real example of the document — one you wrote and someone accepted — and tell the AI to follow that exact structure for new content.** The old document carries the format, the vocabulary and the house style, so the AI does not have to guess.

## The format-following prompt

```
ROLE: You are an experienced [your role] in a Nigerian [ministry/school/
business/organisation].

CONTEXT: I need to draft a new [document type]. Below is an OLD example
of the same document type from my workplace, which was accepted as
correct. New content for the draft is also below.

TASK: Follow the old example's exact structure — same headings, same
order, same style of numbering — but use only the new content. Do not
copy information from the old example. Do not invent anything.

OLD EXAMPLE:
[paste it]

NEW CONTENT:
[paste your notes]
```

## The template-extraction move

This is the one you will thank yourself for learning. One prompt turns any good old document into a reusable fill-in template:

```
Study the document below and turn it into a reusable template: keep its
exact structure and headings, but replace the specific information with
clearly marked fill-in slots like [DATE], [RECIPIENT], [SUBJECT].
Add one line under each slot saying what goes there. Output the template
only.

DOCUMENT:
[paste your good old document]
```

Paste the result into your phone notes once. From today, every future document of that type starts from your own office's real format, not the internet's generic one.

## Tone control

Same content, three registers — and each has its place in Nigerian workplaces:

- **Official:** for permanent secretaries, regulators, formal correspondence. Full phrases, no contractions. "I wish to respectfully submit…"
- **Concise:** for busy colleagues and internal notes. Short sentences, bullets, the ask up front.
- **Warm:** for customers, community members, parents. Greetings, human phrasing, "we appreciate…"

Switch with one line appended to any prompt: "Rewrite this more official / more concise / more warm, keeping the structure."

## 🎯 Task

1. Choose the ONE document you write most often.
2. Find one good old example — one that was accepted without corrections.
3. Run the template-extraction prompt on it. Save the template to your phone notes.
4. Draft a fresh document you actually owe someone this week: paste the template plus your notes, and use the format-following prompt.
5. Adjust the tone to match the recipient, using the tone-adjuster line.

## 📤 Output

A saved template in your notes plus one finished, ready-to-send document. Both go in your Playbook. The template is the single most reusable thing you will build this week.
