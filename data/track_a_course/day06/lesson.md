# Your Next Move

**Read time: 6 minutes (your track only) · Task: 20 minutes**

Everything you have learned this week (give real material, say who it is for, name the format, check the output) now goes towards one practical next step in your own situation. This lesson splits into three tracks. Read only the track that matches your work. The task is a real piece of work you owe someone, done faster.

## Track A: CV, cover letter and interview preparation (jobseekers and professionals)

**1. Turn duties into achievements.** Your CV says "responsible for filing records". It should say what changed because you did the work. Paste your CV with this prompt:

> You are an experienced Nigerian HR officer. Here is my CV: [PASTE]. Rewrite each work experience bullet so it describes an achievement, not a duty. Keep it truthful: do not invent numbers I have not given you. Ask me up to 3 questions if you need facts to complete any bullet.

**2. Tailor one CV to one real advert.** In the same chat, paste the job advert and ask:

> Here is the job advert: [PASTE]. Compare it with my CV above. List the top 5 requirements they want, then rewrite my CV profile (the short paragraph at the top) and my bullet points to match, using only true things from my CV.

**3. Write a cover letter that sounds like you.** Keep it short, specific and under one page. Ask for a cover letter that names the exact role, one real achievement, and why you want to work for this employer. Then edit it yourself so it sounds like you, not the AI.

**4. Rehearse the interview.** Paste the job description and use this prompt:

> You are the hiring manager for this role. Ask me 5 likely interview questions one at a time. After each of my answers, tell me one thing that was strong and one thing to improve. Be direct.

**5. Run the job-scam check before you apply.** AI can help you check, but these rules are fixed:

- No genuine employer asks you to **pay a recruitment fee**, pay for "training materials", or buy airtime to "confirm interest". If any fee is requested, walk away.
- Check that the company is registered: search for its name on the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) public search at **search.cac.gov.ng**. If it does not appear, ask the recruiter for the registered name and check again before you share any documents. Then look for a real website and a real person you can call. Be suspicious of a Gmail address doing the hiring for a big company.
- A salary far above the market rate for the role, with an easy interview, is the oldest bait there is.

## Track B: proposals and concept notes (NGO, programme and development staff)

**1. Start from the concept-note skeleton.** Most donors, and your own organisation, expect the same basic structure: problem, objectives, activities, budget lines, and monitoring and evaluation. First, extract your organisation's format from an old document (the Day 3 template-extraction prompt), then draft new sections to fit it.

**2. Use the donor's language.** Paste the call for proposals or the donor's priority text with this prompt:

> Here is the donor's call: [PASTE]. Here is my draft section: [PASTE]. Rewrite my section using the donor's own priority language and phrases, without inventing activities we have not planned or figures we do not have.

**3. Draft the logframe with AI.** Ask the AI to draft a simple results table (activities, outputs, indicators, means of verification), then check every indicator yourself. The AI proposes; you decide.

**4. Turn field data into reportable results.** Paste anonymised field notes and ask: "Summarise this into 5 reportable results with numbers, using only the figures in my notes."

## Track C: lesson plans and teaching materials (teachers and lecturers)

**1. Write the lesson plan in your school's format.** Paste one old approved lesson plan and give the topic for next week:

> Here is my school's lesson plan format: [PASTE]. Prepare next week's lesson for [CLASS] on [TOPIC] in exactly this format, with differentiation for weak learners in the development stage.

**2. Prepare differentiated notes.** For mixed-ability classes, ask for the same lesson at three levels: the core explanation, a simpler version with a local example, and an extension task for fast finishers.

**3. Give marking feedback that is specific and kind.** Paste anonymised student answers with this prompt:

> Here are students' answers on [TOPIC] (anonymised). For each, write feedback with one specific thing done well and one specific improvement. Kind but honest tone. Then give me 3 whole-class teaching points from the common errors.

**4. Create revision questions and marking guides.** Paste your syllabus or scheme of work and ask for revision questions with answers. Then check each answer before you teach it. In a classroom, the Day 2 4-question check is not optional.

## Worked examples for the three tracks

*These are illustrative teaching examples, not learner results.*

- **Jobseeker:** Input: "Filed weekly stock records and followed up missing entries; no measured improvement recorded." Prompt: "Write one truthful CV bullet. Do not invent a percentage." Sample: "Maintained weekly stock records and followed up missing entries." Check that you really did both things. An honest duty is better than an invented achievement.
- **Programme officer:** Input: "Two sessions held; 18 attendees at the first and 16 at the second; repeat attendance unknown." Prompt: "Draft one results sentence and identify the counting limitation." Sample: "Two sessions recorded 34 attendances; the number of unique participants has not been established." Check the attendance registers before claiming that 34 people were reached.
- **Teacher:** Input: "Primary 4; fractions; 35 minutes; bottle tops available." Prompt: "Create an introduction, guided activity and three assessment questions using bottle tops. Do not assume internet access." Sample activity: arrange eight bottle tops and identify one-half and one-quarter. Check the answers yourself and match the activity to your approved scheme of work.

## 🎯 Task

Follow your track:

- **Track A:** tailor your CV to one real advert and run one mock interview. Type your answers in the chat rather than only thinking them through.
- **Track B:** draft one real section of a proposal or report you owe this month.
- **Track C:** produce next week's lesson plan, or marking feedback for one real set of anonymised student work.

## 📤 Output

One finished work product from your own job: the kind of thing that would normally take you an evening, done in minutes and checked by you. Save it in your phone notes or documents and keep it for your Playbook (Day 7).
