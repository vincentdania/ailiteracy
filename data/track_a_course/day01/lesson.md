# From Meeting Notes to a Finished Report in 10 Minutes

**Read time: 8 minutes · Task: 10–15 minutes**

This course is about your work, not AI theory. By the end of today you will have turned rough notes into a finished, formatted report: a useful draft that you then check against your notes.

## What this course is and is not

It **is** one short lesson and one real task every day, using the work you already have on your desk or in your phone.

It **is not** coding, installing software or a lecture about how AI works. You will never be asked to write a line of code. Everything runs on free tools (the free tier of ChatGPT or Gemini) on the phone or laptop you already own.

The course makes no promises of income or job placement. What it does promise is simpler: every day, you finish with something you made yourself.

## Opening ChatGPT or Gemini in under a minute

1. Open your phone or laptop browser and go to **chatgpt.com** or **gemini.google.com**.
2. Tap **Sign up** or **Log in** (ChatGPT) or **Sign in** (Gemini) and use your email address or Google account. Choose the free option. You do not need a bank card or a download, although the apps work too.
3. You should now see a typing box at the bottom of the screen. That box is where you will work for the next ten days. If you are offered a paid plan, close that screen or choose the free option.

## The three ideas you need today

1. **Give the AI your real material.** Paste your actual notes, not a vague description of them. The AI cannot read your mind, and a worker with empty hands can do nothing.
2. **Say exactly who the output is for.** "For my Head of Service", "for parents" or "for a customer" changes the words the AI chooses.
3. **Tell it the format you want.** Headings? Bullet points? How long? If you don't say, it guesses, and its guesses are generic.

The rest of this course builds on these three ideas.

## The worked demo

Below is an illustrative meeting scenario, a reusable prompt and a sample result. It is not a record of a real meeting.

**The messy notes:**

> staff meeting 14 Aug, 9 people present. new resumption time 8am from Sept, Mrs Eze to circulate memo. borehole repair quoted 380k, wait for 2nd quote. Friday clean-up continues. Ada to follow up on training forms, 6 staff yet to submit. AOB: NEPA bill unpaid, threat of disconnection, Mr Bala handling.

**The exact prompt used:**

```
You are an experienced administrative officer. Below are my rough notes
from a staff meeting. Turn them into a clean report for my Head of
Department.

Use this format:
- A one-line heading with the meeting date
- "Attendees:" line
- "Matters arising" as short numbered points
- "Action points" as a list in this form: [task]: [owner name]

Keep it under 200 words. Do not invent anything that is not in the notes.

NOTES:
[paste your notes here]
```

**What came back** was a report with a dated heading, an attendees line, numbered matters arising and a list of action points naming Mrs Eze, Ada and Mr Bala against their tasks. You can see a sample of this kind of result further down. The whole job took one paste and a few minutes of checking.

## The same template, six different jobs

One template serves every role. The notes change; the structure does not.

- **Civil servant:** paste your meeting minutes → an official report for the Permanent Secretary.
- **Teacher:** paste staff-briefing notes → a clean briefing summary for colleagues who were absent.
- **SME owner:** paste notes from a customer call → a summary of what was agreed, for your records and theirs.
- **NGO officer:** paste field-visit notes → an activity report for your programme officer.
- **Banker:** paste branch meeting notes → a formatted update for your team lead.
- **Jobseeker:** paste notes from a career fair → a summary of who you met, what they said and your follow-ups.

## On a phone and a limited data budget

Write your notes offline first and connect only when you are ready to paste. Use plain text rather than large photos or PDFs, and copy longer documents in labelled sections. Save useful prompts and outputs in your notes app. Free services can have usage limits or outages: if you hit a limit, save your work and come back later. Internet data still costs money, even when the AI service is free. You do not need a paid subscription for any exercise, but features and availability can change.

## Illustrative sample report (not a real meeting record)

**Staff meeting, 14 August**

**Attendees:** Nine people (names not recorded in the notes).

**Matters arising:**

1. Resumption time will be 8 am from September.
2. The borehole repair has been quoted at ₦380,000; a second quotation is awaited.
3. Friday clean-ups continue.
4. Six staff have not yet submitted training forms.
5. The electricity bill is unpaid and there is a threat of disconnection.

**Action points:**

- Circulate the resumption memo: Mrs Eze.
- Follow up on training forms: Ada.
- Handle the unpaid electricity bill: Mr Bala.
- Obtain a second borehole quotation: owner not recorded.

**Check:** The notes do not name the nine attendees or say who will get the second quotation, so the report must not invent either. Before practising with your own workplace notes, remove any identifying details.

## 🎯 Task

Use notes from a real meeting, class or customer conversation you attended. They can be in your phone, in your notes app or in a notebook (type them up first). Then:

1. Open ChatGPT or Gemini (free).
2. Copy the prompt above, replace `[paste your notes here]` with your own notes, and send it.
3. Read the report it produces.
4. Send these follow-up prompts one at a time and see how the report changes: "Make it shorter", "Make it more formal", "Add action points".
5. Compare the final report with what you would have written by hand. Note where it is right and where it left something out or added something that is not in your notes. Tomorrow you will learn how to check for this.

## 📤 Output

One finished report made from your own notes. Copy it into your notes app or a document and keep it: it becomes the first page of your Personal AI Playbook on Day 7. Save the prompt in your phone notes too, because you will reuse it often.
