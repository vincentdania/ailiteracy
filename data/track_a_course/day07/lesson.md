# Your Personal AI Playbook

**Read time: 10 minutes · Task: 10 minutes**

Six days of work are sitting in your chat history, and they disappear from view the moment you open a new conversation. Today you gather them into one document that stays with you: your **Personal AI Playbook**.

## The Playbook format

Your Playbook is one note or document on your phone (your notes app is fine) with these six sections:

- **Reports:** your meeting-notes-to-report template from Day 1, plus your decisions-summary prompt from Day 5.
- **Emails & messages:** your ask-in-two-lines email skeleton and your customer-reply set from Day 4.
- **Numbers:** your spreadsheet-builder prompt from Day 5.
- **My-role documents:** the format-following and template-extraction prompts from Day 3, plus your Day 6 work product.
- **My privacy rules:** the "safe to paste / never paste" lists from Day 2, in your own words.
- **My verification checklist:** the four questions from Day 2. Does it name a source? Does the source exist? Do the numbers add up? Would I say this to my boss with my name on it?

Keep it plain: a note with those six headings and your actual prompts under each.

## The 10-minute Playbook review ritual

Once a week, on the same day at the same time, spend ten minutes on this:

1. Look at what AI produced for you this week.
2. **Keep** what worked. Make sure the prompt behind it is saved in the Playbook.
3. **Fix one weak prompt.** If an output missed the mark, add one sentence of instruction to that prompt so it works better next time.

The third step is the habit that makes this course last. AI tools change every month, but a Playbook you improve every week keeps getting better.

## Keeping current without chasing hype

You will hear about a new AI tool every few weeks. You can ignore most of them. Tools change, but the three moves stay the same: give real material, specify role and format, and verify the output. Every new tool you meet works with those same three moves. Judge a new tool by testing it with your Playbook prompts, not by the excitement around it.

## Where to go next

From here, what improves your results is **practice**: the weekly ritual above, run on your real work. The next three days (Days 8–10, Agents Week, included in this course at no extra cost) show you how to turn your Playbook into an assistant with standing instructions, memory and a weekly routine.

## A filled Playbook page you can adapt

*An illustrative example for a small stationery shop. Replace it with your own work.*

**Reports:** "From my weekly notes, list completed orders, unresolved issues and next actions. Do not invent sales or customer feedback."

**Emails & messages:** "Write a polite reply under 60 words using only my confirmed stock, prices and collection arrangements. Ask for missing details; never promise a delivery I have not confirmed."

**Numbers:** "Create Item, Quantity, Unit price and Total columns. Explain the formulas. Flag missing costs and keep sales revenue separate from profit."

**My-role documents:** "Draft a quotation with item, quantity, price, validity and collection terms. Use only the details I supply; mark any missing field as 'not supplied'."

**My privacy rules:** No customer phone numbers, addresses, payment details or private conversations. Use made-up records for practice.

**My verification checklist:** Check every price, quantity and deadline against my records. Verify any outside source. Remove invented claims. Approve the message myself before sending.

The shop owner would test the customer-reply prompt on one enquiry and the spreadsheet prompt on two rows, then save the corrected prompts, not the first versions.

## 🎯 Task

1. Assemble your Playbook in one note or document, with all six sections and your real prompts under each.
2. Choose **two** prompts from it. Run each one again in a new chat, on real (or anonymised) material, and check the result.
3. If a result disappoints, add one sentence of instruction to that prompt and save the corrected version in your Playbook.
4. Submit the Playbook in the "Show your real work" form on this lesson page. In the description, list your six sections, name the two prompts you tested and say what you changed. If your Playbook is in Google Docs, you can also add a view-only link.
