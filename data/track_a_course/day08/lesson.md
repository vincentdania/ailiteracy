# What an Agent Actually Is

**Read time: 6 minutes · Task: 15 minutes**

A chatbot answers. An **agent** works: on instructions, with memory, on a schedule. Today you learn the four parts of an agent, see the difference for yourself, and turn your Playbook into a standing assistant.

## The four parts of an agent

A chatbot answers a question and stops. An agent has four things a chatbot does not:

1. **Standing instructions:** its role and rules, stated once and applied every time.
2. **Memory:** your context, your templates, your way of writing.
3. **Tools:** what it may use (a document, a spreadsheet, a calendar).
4. **A trigger:** when it works, such as every Monday, before a meeting, or when you ask.

There is nothing mysterious about this. It is what a good personal assistant is: briefed once, remembers the job, knows the tools, shows up on time.

## Harness, in plain words

Every working agent sits inside **scaffolding** that holds its instructions, memory and routine together. Engineers call this a harness. You have been building one all week: your Playbook is the start of a harness, with your instructions, templates, rules and checks written down and kept on your phone.

## See one working

There is no recording to watch. You build the demonstration yourself, in about five minutes, on your own phone. Fill in the persona block below, paste it into a new chat, and add one real work request. Then open another new chat and send the **same** request with no persona. Compare the two answers.

The second answer comes from a chatbot. The first comes from an assistant that arrived already briefed. What you build today and tomorrow is the free-tools version: the same four parts, on your own phone.

## The conversion move

Today's move: turn your Playbook into a short instruction set, so every new chat starts already briefed.

**Method 1: paste the persona (the main method, works on any phone).** Fill in the persona block below and save it as the first item in your Playbook. Each time you open a new work chat, paste the block in first, then add your request below it. One extra paste, and the AI arrives briefed.

**Method 2: platform settings (optional).** ChatGPT has *custom instructions* and Gemini has *Saved Info*, both found in the app's settings. Whether these are available on the free tier, and how much text they hold, varies, so check your app. If you find the feature, paste your filled-in persona block into it and save. If you cannot find it, use Method 1.

**Copy-paste persona block** (replace each part in square brackets with your own details):

> You are my work assistant. My role: [YOUR ROLE, e.g. admin officer in a state ministry / small business owner / classroom teacher]. My rules: [YOUR PRIVACY RULES: what I never paste; always flag facts I must verify before I use them]. My formats: [YOUR OUTPUT FORMATS, e.g. memos in my ministry format, customer replies in a warm tone, reports with headings and action points]. When I give you a request, follow my formats and rules without me repeating them. Ask me one clarifying question if my request is genuinely unclear.

Your Playbook note is the assistant's memory. When a task needs more context, paste the relevant section (a template or your customer-reply set) into the chat.

## Worked example: briefing a shop assistant

*Illustrative only. This is a briefed chat, not a connected agent that acts on its own.*

Input: "I sell stationery. Confirmed stock today: blue pens only. Collection from the shop; no delivery service." Prompt: "Act as my drafting assistant. Never invent stock or delivery. Reply to: Do you have red pens and can you deliver?"

Sample output: "We currently have blue pens, not red ones. You can collect from the shop; we do not offer delivery. Would blue pens suit you?"

Check the stock before sending. The chat has not read your inventory, sent a message or booked a delivery. You supplied its context, and you remain responsible for what happens next.

## 🎯 Task

1. Fill in the persona block with your own role, rules and formats, and save it at the top of your Playbook.
2. Install it: paste it into custom instructions or Saved Info if your app has them, or plan to paste it at the start of each new chat (the main method, on any phone).
3. Open a new chat and run one real work request with the persona in place.
4. Open another new chat and send the same request with no persona (or compare with a similar request from Day 1). Note what you no longer had to repeat.

## 📤 Output

One briefed run: the same AI, now carrying your role, rules and formats from the first line, with your filled-in persona block saved in your Playbook. This is the foundation for tomorrow, when you give your assistant a standing job.
