# Day 14 — Your Personal Assistant

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 15 minutes**

You've taught AI your tools. Now teach it your job — and give yourself a deputy who's always available, never tired, and knows how you like things done.

## From generic to personal

A generic chat is a stranger. An assistant is someone briefed on your world. The difference is a **written spec**: who you are, what you do, how you write, what your day looks like. Put the core in custom instructions and keep the full spec as a file. Then any tool — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — becomes an assistant that knows your role, and you can paste the spec into new tools the day you meet them.

## The assistant spec

Four sections are enough:

- **Role** — "I'm a project manager at a construction firm; I report to the managing director"
- **Style** — "plain English, short sentences, bullet points for decisions, no buzzwords"
- **Routines** — "every morning: yesterday's open items, today's meetings, three priorities"
- **Know-how** — the facts you re-explain constantly: team names, clients, current projects, deadlines

That last section is the gold. Every fact you type once is a fact you never retype.

## The daily briefing routine

The routine is the point. Each morning, one prompt: *"Here's my plan for today: [paste]. Give me anything I've missed, my three priorities, and what I should say no to."* ChatGPT's scheduled Tasks — on paid plans — can run this automatically; on free plans, a calendar reminder to paste your day works just as well. The value isn't the automation; it's the habit of planning the day in one place, with a second pair of eyes.

## Why this matters

Your assistant spec is the document you'll reuse forever. New tool, new job, new team — paste the spec, update the facts, and you're working within a day. It's the closest thing to hiring a deputy without the salary. And because you wrote it, it carries your judgment, not a template's.

---

## 🎯 Task (15 minutes)

Write your **personal assistant spec**.

1. Four sections: role (2–3 lines), style (3–5 rules), routines (morning briefing, weekly review), know-how (names, projects, deadlines).
2. Save it as a file — this is your assistant prompt file.
3. Test it: paste the spec into any tool and run one routine, like today's briefing; refine until the output is useful.

*Example: "You are my assistant. I'm a sales lead at a fintech. Style: direct, no hype. Morning: list open deals, flag anything urgent, propose today's top three calls."*

## 📤 Output

Your **assistant prompt file**. Share a line from it in the community — the role and style lines are the ones everyone steals.
