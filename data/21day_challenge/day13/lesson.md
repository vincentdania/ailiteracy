# Day 13 — Co-Writing Real Deliverables

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 15 minutes**

A draft from AI is easy. A finished document that sounds like you — that's a skill, and it's the one that gets you noticed. Today you build it.

## The working document method

The mistake is treating AI like a vending machine: one prompt, one final answer. Instead, create a **working document** — a Claude Artifact is ideal — and build it in rounds: outline, rough draft, tighten, polish. Each round, you read, mark what's wrong, and send it back with specific instructions: *"make the intro punchier"*, *"section three repeats section one — cut it"*, *"this paragraph sounds like a robot; rewrite it in my voice."* The document improves in place, so there's never a version-control mess.

## Iterative editing beats big rewrites

Small, specific instructions beat vague ones every time. "Shorten by 20%" works; "make it better" doesn't. If the voice is off, give it an example: *"here's how I'd say it — match this."* After two or three rounds, the draft starts to sound like you, because you've been steering the whole way. That's co-writing: it brings the speed; you bring the judgement.

## Enforce your style guide

Paste your style rules in once and every draft obeys them:

- **Vocabulary** — "we say customers, never clients"
- **Voice** — "active voice, short sentences"
- **Conventions** — "no acronyms without spelling them out"
- **Tone** — "confident, no hype words"

Organisations spend fortunes on brand voice; you can enforce yours with one paragraph of instructions. When a round lands well, copy it out to Word or Google Docs and keep the artifact as the working copy.

## Why this matters

Deliverables are where your reputation lives. Co-writing is not delegation — it's a partnership where you supply judgement and it supplies speed. The pro's tell-tale sign: you can't tell AI was involved, because the voice is yours. That's the bar. A reader should never spot "this paragraph was generated" — they should just notice you got faster.

## 🎯 Task (15 minutes)

Co-write **one real deliverable** — a proposal, a report section, a policy note, a client update.

1. Outline it in the project you built yesterday.
2. Draft it as an artifact, then run **2–3 edit rounds** with specific instructions.
3. Copy the final version out and read it aloud once — fix anything that isn't you.

*Example: "Draft our quarterly client update. Audience: non-technical. Tone: confident, no hype. Then cut it by 20% and rewrite the opening in a warmer voice."*

## 📤 Output

Your **final document**. If it's safe to share, post a before/after in the community — your old process versus the co-written version. Before/afters are the best proof of progress.
