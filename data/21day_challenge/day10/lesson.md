# Day 10 — Presentations and Visuals

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 15 minutes**

Presentations used to cost you an evening. Now they cost minutes — if you let AI do the heavy lifting and keep the thinking for yourself.

## Start with the outline, not the slides

Never open a blank slide deck again. Tell AI the topic, the audience, the duration, and the **one message** you want people to remember, and ask for a slide-by-slide outline. Approve the story before any design happens. The story is the part only you own — AI suggests the structure, but you decide what matters. A 10-minute executive update and a 45-minute training session need completely different outlines; say which one you're building.

## Let a deck tool do the design

With the outline approved, feed it to an AI deck tool — Gamma, Tome, or Canva's AI tools are the popular ones, and most have free tiers. The tool turns your outline into designed slides in minutes, and you keep editing control, so treat the output as a first design, not a finished deck. The tool picks the layout; you supply the thinking. If it looks generic, that's a signal to add your specifics — your numbers, your examples, your photos.

## Generate the visuals

Most assistants — ChatGPT and Gemini included — can generate images right in the chat, with limits on free tiers. Ask for *"a clean, minimal illustration for a slide about X"* and you'll get something usable. Keep it simple: one image per slide, plain backgrounds, no cluttered clip-art energy.

## Charts from your data

For charts, don't guess. Paste your numbers and ask which chart type tells the story best — a bar chart for comparison, a line for trends, a single big number for impact — plus a one-line takeaway to put on the slide. The takeaway line is what belongs on the slide; the chart is the evidence.

## Why this matters

Nobody gets promoted for making beautiful slides. People get promoted for communicating clearly. AI collapses the design time, which frees you to spend your effort on the thinking — the argument, the evidence, the order. One credibility rule: **check every number** that appears on a slide. A confident-sounding wrong figure is worse than no figure, because your name is on the deck. Let AI build the slides; you own the story.

## 🎯 Task (15 minutes)

Pick a presentation you actually need — a team update, a proposal, a training session.

1. Get a slide-by-slide outline from AI (topic, audience, duration, one key message).
2. Generate **2 supporting visuals**: a chart from real data, or a simple generated image.
3. Read the outline for the story and fix anything that doesn't sound like you.

*Example: "Outline a 10-minute update for my manager on our Q3 results. Audience: executive. One message: we're on track, but we need more marketing budget."*

## 📤 Output

Your **deck outline plus 2 visuals**. Post the outline in the community if you're happy with it — and steal a colleague's outline technique in return.
