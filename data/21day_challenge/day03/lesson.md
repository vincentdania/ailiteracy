# Day 3 — The Art of the Prompt

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 10 minutes**

The difference between a useless answer and a brilliant one is rarely the AI. It's the prompt. Today you learn the four-part framework that gets consistently good results from any tool, every time.

## Weak prompt, weak answer

"Write a report on customer feedback." What report? For whom? How long? The AI has to guess — and guessing is where generic, useless answers come from. The fix is not a magic phrase. It's four parts.

## The 4-Part Framework

**Role + Context + Task + Format.** Every strong prompt contains all four:

- **Role** — who the AI is playing: "You are an experienced customer success manager..."
- **Context** — what it must know: the situation, the audience, the constraints.
- **Task** — the one thing you want done, stated as a verb.
- **Format** — how the answer should look: five bullet points, a table, a one-page memo.

## Weak vs strong

Weak: "Summarise this meeting."

Strong: "You are my executive assistant. We had a 45-minute team meeting; here are the notes. Summarise it in five bullet points for my manager, who missed the meeting. Plain English, no jargon, no names."

Same request. One gets mush, one gets a deliverable. The second took ten extra seconds to type.

## Constraints are the secret

Tone ("formal but warm"), audience ("for a client who is not technical"), length ("under 200 words"), exclusions ("do not mention pricing"). Constraints aren't nagging — they're the difference between an answer that could be for anyone and an answer that's for you.

## The edit loop

Your first answer is a draft, not a verdict. Follow up: "Make it shorter." "More direct." "Give me a better example." "That's not what I meant — here's the real situation." Editing beats restarting, every time.

## Why this matters

The prompt is the only part of AI you control. Everyone else has the same tools — the people who get excellent work are the ones who ask excellent questions. That's the skill you're building today, and it pays off on every single day of this course.

---

## 🎯 Task (10 minutes)

Pick 3 weak prompts you actually use at work — "Write an email", "Summarise this", "Give me some ideas" — and rewrite each with all four parts. Write each before/after pair side by side.

*Example: "Before: 'Draft a proposal.' After: 'You are a commercial proposal writer. The client is a Lagos retail chain considering our inventory software; they care about cost savings and downtime. Draft a one-page proposal, formal tone, ending with three next steps.'"*
