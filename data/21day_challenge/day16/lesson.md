# Day 16 — What AI Agents Actually Are

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 10 minutes**

So far, every tool you've used has one thing in common: you ask, it answers. Today, the game changes. You're going to meet AI that doesn't just talk — it acts.

## Chat answers. Agents do.

A chatbot waits for your prompt, replies, and stops. An agent takes an instruction and keeps going: it checks sources, runs steps, produces something, and reports back. Think of the difference between asking someone about your business plan and asking someone to write it.

The honest definition: an agent is a prompt plus tools plus steps, run on a schedule or triggered by an event, with you in charge of the result.

## What that looks like in practice

- **Scheduled research** — every morning at 8am, AI gathers the day's news on your industry and drafts a five-bullet briefing
- **Report pipelines** — raw sales data goes in, a formatted weekly report comes out
- **Automated follow-ups** — after every meeting, a draft follow-up email with action items
- **Monitoring** — an alert when a price, competitor page, or job listing changes

Notice the pattern: none of these are one-off questions. They're jobs with steps, handed to a machine that repeats them without complaining.

## The supervision rule

Here's the part the hype leaves out: agents are still the same prediction engines you've been using. Fast, eager, sometimes wrong — now working between your checks. If a chatbot gives you a wrong number, you spot it. If an agent builds a whole report on a wrong number, the error is buried deeper.

So the rule is simple: **start supervised, stay supervised.** Use agents for work you would check anyway — drafts, summaries, reminders. Review their output like an intern's, because that's what they are.

## What not to hand over yet

- Money movements and payments
- Anything with your passwords or secrets
- Final decisions with real consequences
- Messages to clients or bosses sent without your review

Agents are early. The professionals winning with them aren't automating everything — they're automating the boring middle and keeping the judgement for themselves.

## Why this matters

Every task you delegate frees hours. Every hour you delegate without checking creates risk. The skill of the next decade isn't building agents — it's deciding what to let them touch.

---

## 🎯 Task (10 minutes)

Identify **3 tasks you do every week** that could run end-to-end without you — with you only checking the result. For each, write:

- The task
- The trigger (when it should run — time, event, new data)
- What you'd check before using the output

*Example: "Competitor update — Monday 9am — check the three sources cited before sharing."*

## 📤 Output

Your **Delegation List**. Three tasks, each with a trigger and a check. Tomorrow you'll build the first one for real — Day 17 is the day your automation goes live.
