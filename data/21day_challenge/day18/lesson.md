# Day 18 — Build Your First Workflow

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 15 minutes**

Yesterday you set one automation loose. Today you connect it to the rest of the job — so a single trigger produces a finished piece of work, not just a first step.

## The five-part pattern

Every good workflow has the same skeleton:

1. **Trigger** — what starts it: a time, a new document, a form response
2. **Gather** — collect what's needed: notes, data, files
3. **Process** — AI does the thinking: summarise, draft, analyse
4. **Output** — deliver the result: document, email, message
5. **Human check** — you review before anything goes anywhere important

Five steps. Only the last one is yours.

## A worked example: meeting follow-up

Say you leave every client call with messy notes and a vague promise to "send a summary".

- **Trigger:** new meeting note saved to your notes app
- **Gather:** the note text and the attendee list
- **Process:** AI extracts decisions and action items, assigns owners
- **Output:** a draft follow-up email to the client
- **Human check:** you read it, correct it, hit send

Same call, one minute of your time instead of twenty.

## Building yours

Start from your Day 17 automation and extend it one step forward and one step back:

- What should **trigger** it automatically instead of you remembering?
- What **input** does it need that you currently collect by hand?
- What **output** would make the result genuinely useful — not just interesting?
- Where is your **checkpoint**?

Sketch it on paper first. Workflows fail on the drawing board, not in the tool — a trigger nobody set up, an input nobody feeds, an output nobody reads. Design the whole job before you wire anything.

## Keep the human in it

The checkpoint is not a weakness; it's the feature. A workflow with a human review at the end can run for months. One without it gets trusted too fast — and an AI that's wrong every day is worse than no AI at all. Check the first five runs closely, then keep checking every run after that.

## Why this matters

A workflow is how you stop doing the same job twice. Every week you spend an hour on something a machine could repeat perfectly. Today is the day you stop.

## 🎯 Task (15 minutes)

Build **one real workflow** for your job, using the five-part pattern:

- Sketch it on paper: trigger → gather → process → output → human check
- Wire it in the tool you used yesterday (ChatGPT Tasks, Claude Projects, or your flow tool)
- Run it end-to-end once, with you doing the final check

*Example: "New invoice received → extract details → draft payment reminder → email to me → I review and send."*
