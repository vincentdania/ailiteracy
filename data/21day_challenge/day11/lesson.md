# Day 11 — Automation Light

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 10 minutes**

Every time you retype the same prompt, you're burning minutes you'll never get back. Today, you stop. This is automation without the code: no pipelines, no tools to break — just smarter defaults.

## Teach AI who you are

**Custom instructions** in ChatGPT are a short profile that applies to every conversation: who you are, how you write, what you never want in an output. Write it once and every chat starts pre-briefed — no more re-explaining your job in the first message. Claude offers similar settings. Three things to include:

- **Who you are** — "I'm a finance manager at a logistics company"
- **How you write** — "short sentences, plain English, no buzzwords"
- **What to avoid** — "never invent numbers — say 'check this' instead"

It's the difference between an intern who needs the whole story every morning and one you briefed on day one.

## Saved prompts and templates

Then save the prompts that work. ChatGPT has saved prompts for this; a plain file in your notes works just as well. Structure your templates like the framework from Day 3 — role, context, task, format — with blanks for the parts that change:

*"You are my editor. Here is my draft: ___. Make it tighter and more persuasive, same meaning, under ___ words."*

Three good templates beat thirty mediocre ones. Every template you save is a prompt you never have to think about again.

## Document your repeatable workflows

Finally, write down the workflows you repeat: the weekly report, the meeting summary, the client email that goes out every Friday. For each, note the prompt you use, the tweaks you always make, and the output format you need. That document is your personal playbook. It takes an hour to build and saves you hours every week — and if you ever switch tools, the playbook comes with you.

## Why this matters

Consistency is what makes work look professional, and templates are consistency on tap. Custom instructions and saved prompts are the cheapest system you'll ever build: set up once, pays out every single day. Because this is "light" automation, there's nothing to monitor and nothing to break — just better starting points for everything you write. Stop retyping the same prompt. Save it once, use it forever.

## 🎯 Task (10 minutes)

1. Write your **custom instructions** — role, style, audience, things to avoid — and switch them on.
2. Save **3 reusable prompt templates** for your most common work tasks, with blanks for the changing parts.
3. Test one template on a real task and tweak it until the output is right.

*Example template: "Draft a reply to this email. Tone: professional but warm. End with a clear next step. Under 120 words: ___"*

## 📤 Output

Your **template library** — three saved, tested templates. Share your best one in the community, and borrow someone else's in return.
