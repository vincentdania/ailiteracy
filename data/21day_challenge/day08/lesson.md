# Day 8 — AI as Thinking Partner

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 15 minutes**

Decisions are where the real money is lost — not in drafting, but in thinking. Today you use AI the way a good consultant works: to structure the problem, test your assumptions, and find what you're missing.

## Pros and cons, done properly

Naive pros and cons lists are shallow. Instead: "List six pros and six cons of [decision], each with a one-line reason and a weight out of ten." Then: "Which three matter most, and why?" The weighting is what turns a list into an analysis.

## Decision matrices

Choosing between options? "I'm deciding between A, B and C. Score each against cost, time, risk and quality, out of ten, with weights for each criterion." You supply the scores you trust; AI does the layout and the arithmetic. Seeing it in a matrix forces the trade-offs into the open.

## SWOT and risk lists

"Run a SWOT on this plan." "List the ten biggest risks, with likelihood, impact, and one mitigation each." These aren't business-school exercises — they're how you catch problems while they're still cheap.

## The devil's advocate

The single most valuable prompt in this course: **"What am I missing?"** Or: "Argue against this decision. What's wrong with it? Who would oppose it, and why?" AI won't flatter you. It will tell you the uncomfortable thing you didn't want to think about — which is exactly what you need before you commit.

## The rule

AI structures your thinking, surfaces blind spots, and makes trade-offs visible. It cannot own the decision — you have the stakes, the relationships, the accountability. The output is a memo; the decision is yours.

## Why this matters

Most bad decisions aren't bad ideas — they're unexamined ones. A thinking partner that never sleeps, never takes sides, and will happily argue against you costs nothing and raises the quality of every call you make. That's not a gimmick; it's a professional advantage.

---

## 🎯 Task (15 minutes)

Take one real decision you're facing this week — a hire, a tool choice, a vendor, a project go/no-go, a job offer. Run it through AI: weighted pros and cons, a decision matrix or SWOT, and at least one "what am I missing?" round. Keep the whole conversation.

*Example: "A real decision, not a hypothetical. If it doesn't keep you up slightly at night, it's too small."*

## 📤 Output

Your **One-Page Decision Memo**: the decision, the options, the analysis, your verdict — and the one thing the devil's advocate raised that you hadn't thought of. Tomorrow you turn to spreadsheets: formulas, cleaning and models — the grunt work AI does best.
