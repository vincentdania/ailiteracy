# Day 19 — Safety, Privacy, Ethics

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 10 minutes**

You now have a machine that drafts, researches, and works on a schedule. Time for the conversation every course skips: how to use it without getting yourself — or someone else — hurt.

## Hallucinations are the baseline, not the bug

AI doesn't know things; it predicts them. Wrong facts come out with total confidence — invented citations, wrong figures, made-up names. This isn't a glitch you can fix with a better prompt. It's the price of admission.

Your defence is one habit you've had since Day 1: **verify what matters.** For anything that will be read, cited, or paid for, check the facts and ask for sources.

## Data privacy: the one hard rule

Never put anything into an AI tool that you wouldn't post publicly — because with some tools, you're effectively doing exactly that. That means:

- No passwords, bank details, or identification numbers
- No confidential client or company data, unless your employer's policy allows it
- No other people's private information without asking

Ask before every paste: *"If this appeared on the internet tomorrow, would anyone be harmed?"* If yes, don't paste it.

## Your workplace's policy

Many organisations already have AI rules — which tools staff may use, what data may be entered, which tasks need approval. Find yours and read it. "I didn't know" has never been a defence that worked.

## Bias and fairness

AI learned from the internet, and the internet is not neutral. Outputs can carry stereotypes about gender, race, age, and nationality — and they sound authoritative while doing it. Watch for it in hiring materials, customer communications, and anything about people. If a draft feels off about a person or a group, fix it before it ships.

## Deepfakes and the trust problem

Realistic fake images, voices, and videos are now cheap to make. The ethical line is simple: don't create them of real people without consent, and don't share them. If a colleague or client gets "caught" in something that seems too real, pause before believing or forwarding.

## What you should never automate

Keep your Day 16 list safety-checked: no automated payments, no secrets in flows, no messages sent without your review, and nothing that could harm someone if it ran wrong for a week while nobody watched.

## Why this matters

Every tool you've built this month is only as safe as the habits around it. The person who gets burned by AI doesn't trip on the technology — they trip on the paste, the unverified number, the thoughtless share. Don't be that person.

## 🎯 Task (10 minutes)

Write your **personal AI safety rules** — a card you can keep on your desk. Include:

- Your **verify** rule (what you always check before trusting output)
- Your **never paste** list (the categories of data that stay out of AI tools)
- Your **checkpoint** rule (what must pass through you before going out)

*Example: "1) Verify every number before sending. 2) Never paste client data or passwords. 3) Nothing leaves my desk without my review."*
