# Day 6 — Writing and Drafting

**Read time: 5 minutes · Task: 15 minutes**

Phase 2. No more practice tasks — from today, AI works on the actual writing you'd otherwise do yourself. Emails, reports, proposals: the words you get paid to produce.

## The sandwich method

**Give context → AI drafts → you edit → AI polishes.**

- **Give context** — who's reading, what you're trying to achieve, the key facts. The four-part framework from Day 3 does this for you.
- **AI drafts** — a 70% draft in seconds. That's the point: starting is the hardest part of writing, and you just skipped it.
- **You edit** — cut, rewrite, make it sound like you. Non-negotiable. This is where your judgment goes in.
- **AI polishes** — send your edited version back: "Tighten this. Strengthen the opening. Remove anything vague."

## Tone controls

AI follows tone instructions well: "formal but not stiff", "warm but professional", "firm without being rude". If a draft sounds wrong, don't restart — adjust: "Make this friendlier." "This is too salesy; make it matter-of-fact." Tone is a dial, not a coin flip.

## Fixing weak writing

The fastest writing upgrade in AI: paste your own weak sentence and ask. "Make this more direct." "Cut it in half." "Say this in simpler words." "Make this more persuasive for a sceptical CFO." You stop wrestling with phrasing and start choosing between versions — and choosing is faster and more fun than staring at a blank page.

## What AI shouldn't write

Anything where tone is deeply personal — a sensitive apology, a disciplinary note, a message where getting it wrong costs a relationship. Draft those yourself; use AI for a second opinion and a polish pass only.

## Why this matters

Writing is the most common work task, so it's the most common AI superpower. Cut drafting from an hour to ten minutes and you don't just save time — you free your best thinking for the parts of the job that need a human. From today, the sandwich is how you write everything.

## 🎯 Task (15 minutes)

Two pieces of real work:

1. Draft one real work email you need to send this week — client, boss or colleague. Run the full sandwich: context, draft, your edit, polish.
2. Draft one section of a real report or proposal — introduction, summary or recommendation.

*Example: "Use real work, not invented scenarios. The point is that today's output leaves your desk."*

## 📤 Output

Your **two polished pieces**: the final email and the final report section, after the full sandwich. Keep the earlier drafts too — the before/after is your proof of how much faster this is.
