**Read time: 10 minutes · Build time: 20 minutes · Cost: included with your Nous account**

The blank page is the slowest part of any proposal. So is the weekly "here's where we are" email that every client expects and nobody enjoys writing.

In this lesson, Hermes writes the first draft of both. You teach it how you write once. After that, you give it a few facts, and it hands back a proposal or an update that sounds like you, ready for you to check.

![A proposal drafted by Hermes: a title, who it's for, the problem and what you'll do.](/lessons/professionals/proposal-client-updates/step-proposal-draft.png "At the end of this lesson, a first draft takes one minute.")

## What you'll have at the end

By the end of this lesson you'll have:

1. **Your writing style**, saved in Hermes's memory.
2. A **first-draft proposal**, built only from your facts, with gaps clearly marked.
3. A **client update** email, saved as a Gmail draft for you to send.
4. A way to send updates **from your phone**, between meetings.

## Before you start

Tick off each item before you go on:

- [ ] **Hermes** is set up and signed in with your **Nous account**.
- [ ] **Gmail is connected.** You did this in the inbox triage lesson.
- [ ] **Two emails you wrote** to clients, that you're happy with. Copy them from your Sent folder.
- [ ] The **facts** for a real proposal: who it's for, their problem, what you'll do, the price and the timeline. Or use the example below to practise.

## Step 1: Teach Hermes how you write

1. Click **New session** at the top left.
2. Copy this request and paste it into the message box:

```text
Here are two emails I wrote to clients. Describe my writing style in five short rules, then remember them for every draft you write for me.
```

3. Press **Shift+Enter** twice for a new line, then paste your two emails underneath. Write `Email 1:` and `Email 2:` above each one.
4. Press **Enter**.

![You share two of your emails (1), Hermes describes your style (2), and saves it to memory (3).](/lessons/professionals/proposal-client-updates/step-your-style.png "Step 1: Hermes learns how you write.")

> **You should now see:** five short rules about how you write, and **Saved to memory**.

**Check the rules.** Do they sound like you? If not, say so: `Rule 2 is wrong. I'm more formal with new clients.` You can change them any time with `Update my writing style`.

> **Stay safe:** Pick emails without private details in them, like bank details, health information or anything a client told you in confidence. Hermes only needs to see how you write, not who you write to.

## Step 2: Draft a proposal from your facts

1. Click **New session**.
2. Copy this request into the message box, and change the client's name to yours:

```text
Draft a proposal for Bright Path Schools in my style. Use only these facts. Mark anything you're not sure of with [CHECK].
```

3. Press **Shift+Enter** twice, then add your facts underneath. Short notes are fine. To practise, use these:

```text
Client: Bright Path Schools, 3 schools in Lagos. Contact: Mrs Ade, Head of Admissions.
Problem: parents can't find admissions information online. Enquiries come by phone and get lost.
What we'll do: website audit, new admissions pages, one enquiry form that goes to one inbox, train 2 staff.
Price: 2.4m naira, 50% upfront.
Timeline: 6 weeks from sign-off.
```

4. Press **Enter**.

![Your request with your facts (1), the title and who it's for (2), and what you'll do (3).](/lessons/professionals/proposal-client-updates/step-proposal-draft.png "Step 2: a first draft, from your facts only.")

> **You should now see:** a proposal with a title and sections: **The problem**, **What we'll do**, **Timeline**, **Price** and **Next steps**. With your own facts, it will look different from the picture.

**The two magic phrases.** "Use only these facts" stops Hermes from filling gaps with things that sound good but aren't true. "Mark anything you're not sure of with [CHECK]" makes every gap easy to spot.

### Have a proposal you always use?

If you have a proposal you like, Hermes can follow its layout. Click the **+** at the left of the message box, click **Files...** and choose it. Then add to your request: `Use the attached proposal as a template for the layout and headings.`

## Step 3: Check every [CHECK]

Scroll to the end of the proposal.

![CHECK marks the gaps (1), a list of what to check (2), and the copy button (3).](/lessons/professionals/proposal-client-updates/step-proposal-check.png "Step 3: Hermes tells you what it didn't know.")

> **You should now see:** **[CHECK]** wherever your facts were missing, and a list called **Before you send, check the [CHECK] items**.

1. **Fill in each gap.** Tell Hermes in plain words: `The second payment is due on handover. The offer is valid for 30 days. Week 1-2 audit, weeks 3-5 pages and form, week 6 training.`
2. **Change anything else.** For example: `Make "The problem" shorter.` or `Add an option without the training, for 2m naira.`
3. **Read it all, slowly.** Check every name, number and date against your facts. Hermes works the sums out from your facts, like 50% of ₦2.4m, but you check them.
4. **Copy it out.** Point at the end of the reply and click the **copy** button. Paste it into your usual document, add your logo, and save it as a PDF to send.

> **Stay safe:** A proposal is a promise. Never send one with a **[CHECK]** still in it, and never let Hermes invent a price, a date or a result. If you see something you didn't give it, ask `Where did that come from?`

## Step 4: Write your weekly client update

Clients love regular updates. Now they take you a minute.

1. Click **New session**.
2. Copy this, **change the notes to your own week**, and send it:

```text
Write this week's update to Mrs Ade at Bright Path Schools in my style, from these notes:
- Website audit finished.
- New admissions pages being written now.
- On track to finish by 4 December.
- I need the 2027 fee list for all three schools by Friday 30 October.
Then save it as a Gmail draft to ade@brightpath.example. Do not send it.
```

If Hermes asks for approval before saving, check what it says it will do, then approve it.

![Your notes (1), the update in your style (2), and saved as a draft, not sent (3).](/lessons/professionals/proposal-client-updates/step-client-update.png "Step 4: notes in, client update out.")

> **You should now see:** a short email in your style, with a clear ask, and "It has **not** been sent".

3. Open **Gmail**, then **Drafts**. Read the update, change anything you need, and click **Send**.

> **If it didn't work:** If Hermes says it can't reach Gmail, go to **Capabilities**, then **Connectors**, and check Gmail says **Connected**. If it says **Access expired**, click **Reconnect**.

## From your phone: updates between meetings

You don't need your laptop. Send your notes to your Hermes bot in Telegram, while Hermes is open on your computer.

![Your notes (1), the update in your style (2), and saved to Gmail, not sent (3).](/lessons/professionals/proposal-client-updates/phone-update.png "Jot it down on your phone. Hermes writes it up.")

1. Open your Hermes bot in Telegram.
2. Type your notes, or hold the **microphone** and say them.
3. End with: `Save it as a Gmail draft. Don't send.`
4. Later, open the Gmail app, check the draft, and press **Send**.

## Tip: never miss an update

Combine this with what you learned in the morning briefing lesson. In **Scheduled jobs** (**Ctrl+K** or **Cmd+K**, type `cron`), create a job for **Fridays at 3pm** with this prompt:

```text
Remind me to send this week's client updates. List my active clients from my memory. Do not send anything.
```

Every Friday, your phone reminds you, and you send your notes to Hermes.

## Keep it safe

1. **Your facts only.** Always say "Use only these facts". Hermes should mark gaps with [CHECK], never fill them.
2. **No [CHECK] left behind.** Search your document for "CHECK" before you send it.
3. **Drafts, not sends.** Hermes saves to Gmail. You read and press Send.
4. **Client confidentiality.** Your facts go to your AI model to be read. Check your clients' contracts allow that, and leave out anything very sensitive. Module 6 covers this in detail.
5. **You own the promise.** Prices, dates and results in a proposal are your decisions. Hermes writes the words around them.
