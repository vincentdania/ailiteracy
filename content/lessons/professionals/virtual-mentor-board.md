**Read time: 8 minutes · Build time: 10 minutes · Cost: included with your Nous account**

Big decisions are lonely when you work for yourself. Should you lower your price? Take on that difficult client? Say yes to the extra work for free? Most of us decide alone, late at night, with nobody to push back.

In this lesson, you'll set up a **mentor board**: four AI advisers, each looking at your problem in a different way. Ask them a question, and you get four points of view, where they agree and disagree, and the questions only you can answer. They challenge you. You decide.

![Your board answers: each adviser's view, where they agree and disagree, and questions only you can answer.](/lessons/professionals/virtual-mentor-board/step-board-answer.png "At the end of this lesson, you'll never decide alone.")

## What you'll have at the end

By the end of this lesson you'll have:

1. A **mentor board** of four advisers, saved in Hermes's memory.
2. A simple way to **ask your board** anything: start a message with `Board:`.
3. Your board **on your phone**, for decisions on the go.

> **New word: persona.** A character the AI plays, with its own point of view. Your board is four personas. They're not real people, and they only know what you tell them.

## Meet your board

Every good decision gets looked at from four sides. That's who's on your board:

| Adviser | Their job | The question they always ask |
|---|---|---|
| 🧭 The Coach | Keeps you true to your goals | "What do you really want?" |
| 💰 The Numbers Person | Checks price, profit, cash and risk | "Does this make money?" |
| 🙋 The Client | Reacts the way your client would | "How will this feel to them?" |
| 🔍 The Sceptic | Argues the other side | "What could go wrong?" |

You can change them. A lawyer might add "The Regulator". A designer might add "The Brand Guardian". Start with these four, and change them once you've tried it.

## Before you start

- [ ] **Hermes** is set up and signed in with your **Nous account**.
- [ ] A **real decision** you're thinking about. Big or small. Something that's been on your mind this week is perfect.

## Step 1: Set up your board

1. Click **New session** at the top left.
2. Copy this request, paste it into the message box and press **Enter**:

```text
Set up my mentor board. I want four advisers who look at my work in different ways:
- The Coach: asks what I really want.
- The Numbers Person: checks price, profit and risk.
- The Client: reacts the way my client would.
- The Sceptic: looks for what could go wrong.
Remember them. When I start a message with "Board:", answer as all four, then sum up where they agree and disagree. Never make the decision for me.
```

![Your request (1), your board (2), and saved to memory (3).](/lessons/professionals/virtual-mentor-board/step-board-setup.png "Step 1: four advisers, set up once.")

> **You should now see:** your four advisers, and **Saved to memory**. You only do this once. Your board is there in every new session, and in Telegram.

**The most important line** is the last one: "Never make the decision for me." It keeps your board honest about its job. It helps you think. It doesn't think for you.

## Step 2: Ask your board a question

1. Start a **New session**, or keep going in this one.
2. Type `Board:` followed by your question. Give the facts that matter: the numbers, the client, what you're worried about. For example:

```text
Board: Bright Path Schools says ₦2.4m is over their budget. Should I offer a cheaper option at ₦2m without the staff training?
```

3. Press **Enter**.

![Your question (1), each adviser's view (2), where they agree and disagree (3), and questions for you (4).](/lessons/professionals/virtual-mentor-board/step-board-answer.png "Step 2: four points of view in one answer.")

> **You should now see:** an answer from each adviser, then **Where they agree**, **Where they disagree**, and **Questions only you can answer**. Scroll up to see the Coach, who answers first.

**Read the disagreements first.** That's where the value is. When the Numbers Person and the Coach disagree, it shows you the real choice you're making.

## Step 3: Go deeper

The first answer is a starting point. Now push back, the way you would with a real mentor:

- **Talk to one adviser:** `Sceptic, what's the worst that could happen if I say yes?`
- **Give a missing fact:** `The training takes me two days. Does that change anything, Numbers Person?`
- **Test a plan:** `Board: here's what I'm thinking of saying to Mrs Ade. What would each of you change?`
- **Answer their questions:** reply to the **Questions only you can answer**. Often, writing the answer is when you realise what you want to do.

## Step 4: Use your board from your phone

Your board works in Telegram too, while Hermes is open on your computer. It's perfect just before a call or a meeting.

![Ask with Board: (1), a short answer from all four (2), and ask one adviser to go deeper (3).](/lessons/professionals/virtual-mentor-board/phone-board.png "A second opinion in your pocket.")

1. Open your Hermes bot in Telegram.
2. Send a message starting with `Board:`.
3. Ask one adviser a follow-up if you want more.

## Change your board

Your board should fit your work. Tell Hermes in plain words:

- **Swap an adviser:** `Update my mentor board: replace the Client with The Regulator, who checks if this is allowed in my industry.`
- **Change how they talk:** `Update my mentor board: keep each adviser to two sentences.`
- **Add a fifth:** `Add an adviser to my board: The Future Me, who asks how I'll feel about this in five years.`

> **Stay safe:** Don't name your advisers after real, living people, like a famous business leader. The AI would be guessing what they think, and putting words in a real person's mouth. Use roles, not names.

## Keep it safe

1. **You decide.** Your board gives views, not answers. Four AI voices are still one AI, and it only knows what you told it.
2. **Check the facts.** If an adviser quotes a number, a law or a market fact, check it before you act on it.
3. **Know when to ask a professional.** For legal, tax, medical or financial decisions with real consequences, use your board to prepare your questions, then speak to a qualified expert.
4. **Client confidentiality.** Describe the situation without details your client wouldn't want shared. "A school client" works as well as their name. Module 6 covers this in detail.
5. **Your private chat only.** Ask your board in your own bot chat. Never add your bot to a group.
