**Week 7 · About 15 to 20 hours · All five competencies**

This week your automation meets real work. With your partner's approval, you'll run a functional pilot on a low-risk process, measure what it really saves and costs, and hand it over.

## What you'll be able to do

- Agree a pilot scope, success measures and stop conditions with a partner
- Move from test copies to live data safely
- Monitor a pilot and respond to issues
- Measure time saved and actual operating costs honestly
- Write handover notes and train the people who'll run it

## Key ideas

**Pilot scope.** A small, clearly bounded trial: one process, a fixed period (for example five working days), named users, and agreed success measures.

**Go-live checklist.** Before switching from test copies to live sheets: tests pass, error workflow is on, access review done, the partner has approved, and you know how to switch it off.

**Stop conditions.** Agreed in advance: "If a wrong amount appears in any draft, pause the workflow and tell the manager."

**Honest measurement.** Time saved is the old time minus the new time, including the time people now spend reviewing drafts and fixing issues. Record operating costs: subscriptions, AI usage and maintenance time.

**Handover.** What it does, how to run it, how to check it worked, what to do when it fails, how to switch it off, who owns each account, and how to remove your access.

## Use AI well this week

Use AI to draft handover notes and user instructions from your own build notes, then test them: ask someone who didn't build it to follow the instructions without your help.

> **Stay safe:** Use only data the partner has authorised for the pilot. No automated payments, no unreviewed external messages and no uploads of sensitive data, however well the pilot goes.

## Your evidence this week

Hand in, as described in the week 7 evidence brief:

1. The signed **pilot plan** and completed **go-live checklist**
2. **Pilot evidence**: the log, issues and how you responded
3. Your **before and after time study** and **operating costs**
4. **Handover notes** and evidence someone else followed them

What the assessor looks for: a safe, approved pilot, honest measurement including new work and costs, and a business that can run the automation without you.

## Before you hand in

- [ ] The partner approved the pilot and go-live in writing
- [ ] Human approval stayed in place for every external communication
- [ ] Time saved accounts for review and fixing time
- [ ] Running costs are recorded, not guessed
- [ ] Someone else followed the handover notes successfully
