**Time: about 2 hours · You'll need a document and a spreadsheet**

Before you learn anything new, you'll show what you can already do. The baseline task takes about 90 minutes and it isn't scored. It tells you and your mentor where to focus over the next eight weeks.

## What you'll have at the end

- A **baseline process diagnosis** for a small business case
- A **self-rating** against the five competencies
- Your **strengths and gaps** written into your competence record

## The competency standard

| Competency | At the foundation standard, you can... |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Map and simplify | Map a workflow as it really happens, find the waste, and simplify it before automating anything |
| 2. Connect safely | Connect spreadsheets and apps so data moves correctly, without duplicates, overwrites or corruption |
| 3. Control and oversight | Design audit logs, exception paths and human review gates, especially before anything leaves the business |
| 4. Test | Test both success and failure scenarios, and show the automation recovers safely |
| 5. Measure honestly | Measure time saved while documenting the real running costs, including subscriptions and maintenance |

Your partner project: automate a **low-risk administrative process**, such as internal status reporting or non-sensitive payment-reminder drafts, with **human approval before any external communication**.

## What this pathway is not

- It doesn't let automations move money, send uncontrolled messages or upload sensitive data.
- It doesn't make you a software engineer. It certifies foundational competence in no-code automation, independently assessed.

## The baseline case

Read this case. It's fictional.

> **Kubwa Print Hub** prints flyers, banners and branded T-shirts in Kubwa, Abuja. Five staff. Orders arrive by WhatsApp, walk-in and email. The front-desk officer writes each order in a notebook, then types it into a Google Sheet "when it's quiet". Every evening the manager phones each designer and printer to ask what's finished, then sends the owner a WhatsApp summary. Customers who owe money are reminded "when someone remembers". The owner says: "We lose orders, chase the same people twice, and I never know what's outstanding. Can we automate everything?"
>
> Known: about 25 orders a day; the evening phone round takes the manager around 45 minutes; roughly 1 in 10 orders has a balance unpaid after delivery. Unknown: how many orders are lost, how much is owed in total, how long data entry takes.

## The baseline task

Spend about 90 minutes. Use an AI assistant if you like, and log how you used it.

Write a two-page diagnosis with these five headings:

1. **The process as it is.** Draw or list the steps of the order process, from order received to payment complete. Mark who does each step and where information is written down.
2. **The pain points.** List at least four problems, and say which ones cause the most cost or risk.
3. **What to simplify first.** At least two changes that need no automation at all.
4. **One automation.** The single automation you'd build first, and why. What triggers it, what it does, and where a person approves.
5. **What could go wrong.** At least three risks of that automation, and how you'd protect against each.

> **Stay safe:** Notice the owner said "automate everything". Part of your job is to say what should *not* be automated, and why.
