**Week 2 · About 15 to 20 hours · Competency 1: Outcomes and scope**

Scope is the line between what the project will and won't do. This week you'll draw that line precisely, break the work into pieces you can plan, and agree how each piece will be accepted.

## What you'll be able to do

- Write a scope statement with deliverables, exclusions, constraints and assumptions
- Build a work breakdown structure (WBS) down to work packages
- Write acceptance criteria for every deliverable
- Explain how scope changes will be controlled

## Key ideas

**Scope statement.** A description of the deliverables, what's excluded, the constraints (fixed date, budget, venue) and the assumptions (learners bring their own phones).

**Work breakdown structure.** Break the project into major deliverables, then into work packages small enough to estimate, assign and track (as a guide, a few days to two weeks of effort). Include project management itself as a branch.

**The 100% rule.** The WBS includes all of the work, and only the work, in scope. If it isn't in the WBS, it isn't in the project.

**Acceptance criteria.** For each deliverable: what "done" means, how it's checked and who signs it off.

**Scope creep.** Uncontrolled additions to scope. Every addition goes through change control (week 6): what's the request, what's the impact, who approves.

## Worked example

```
WBS: Karu Digital Skills Week, Phase 1

1. Project management
   1.1 Charter and plan    1.2 Weekly status reports    1.3 Closure and lessons learned
2. Curriculum and materials
   2.1 Session outlines (4)    2.2 Handouts (English and Hausa)    2.3 Practical check
3. People
   3.1 Trainer recruitment and agreements    3.2 Volunteer helpers    3.3 Trainer briefing
4. Venue and logistics
   4.1 Hall booking    4.2 Power backup (generator hire and fuel)    4.3 Refreshments
5. Learners
   5.1 Outreach and registration    5.2 Attendance tracking    5.3 Session feedback
6. Delivery
   6.1 Sessions 1–4, group A    6.2 Sessions 1–4, group B    6.3 Practical checks
7. Reporting
   7.1 Attendance and results analysis    7.2 Report to bank
```

```
ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA (extract)
Deliverable          | Done when...                                                       | Checked by
2.2 Handouts         | 4 handouts, English and Hausa, 70 copies each, reviewed for accuracy| Lead trainer, chair
4.2 Power backup     | Generator tested at the hall the day before session 1; fuel for 8 sessions | Coordinator
5.1 Registration     | ≥ 60 adults registered with name, phone, group; consent to contact recorded | Coordinator
7.2 Report to bank   | Attendance, pass rate, feedback summary, spend vs budget; approved by chair | Chair, bank lead
```

## Use AI well this week

AI can draft a first WBS quickly. Your job is to check it against the 100% rule and the scope boundaries.

```
Draft a work breakdown structure for: [paste scope statement, no personal data].
Three levels. Include project management. Then list anything you included that
is outside the scope statement, and anything in the scope statement you couldn't
place.
```

> **Stay safe:** If AI adds deliverables outside the agreed scope, remove them or raise them with the sponsor. Don't let a tool quietly expand the project.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** Write the scope statement for your project. Build the WBS on sticky notes or in a spreadsheet, then check every work package against the scope statement. Write acceptance criteria for each major deliverable, and review them with the sponsor.

## Your evidence this week

Hand in, as described in the week 2 evidence brief:

1. A **scope statement** with deliverables, exclusions, constraints and assumptions
2. A **WBS** to work-package level
3. **Acceptance criteria** for each major deliverable, with who checks them

What the assessor looks for: complete and bounded scope, a WBS that follows the 100% rule, and criteria that are specific and checkable.

## Before you hand in

- [ ] Exclusions, constraints and assumptions are written down
- [ ] Every work package fits within the scope statement
- [ ] Project management work is included in the WBS
- [ ] Every major deliverable has checkable acceptance criteria and a named checker
- [ ] The sponsor has reviewed the scope
