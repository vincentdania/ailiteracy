**Week 5 · About 15 to 20 hours · Competency 4: Adapt**

There's no single right way to deliver a project. This week you'll compare predictive, agile and hybrid approaches, choose one for your project with reasons, and set up a backlog. You'll also formalise how you review anything AI produces for your plans.

## What you'll be able to do

- Explain predictive, agile and hybrid delivery, and when each fits
- Choose an approach based on uncertainty, constraints and stakeholders
- Write and order a backlog of user stories with acceptance criteria
- Keep an AI review log for planning outputs

## Key ideas

**Predictive.** Plan the whole project up front, then deliver in phases. Good when scope is clear and stable, constraints are fixed, and changes are costly (a venue booking, a funder's deadline).

**Agile.** Deliver in short cycles (often one or two weeks), get feedback, and adapt. Work is held in an ordered backlog. Good when needs are uncertain or will be discovered by doing.

**Hybrid.** Predictive for the fixed parts, agile for the uncertain parts. Most small community and business projects end up here.

| Factor | Points to predictive | Points to agile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requirements | Clear, stable | Unclear, evolving |
| Cost of change | High (bookings, contracts) | Low (content, sequence) |
| Feedback | Only possible at the end | Available every cycle |
| Constraints | Fixed date, budget, regulation | Flexible scope |

**User stories.** A simple way to write backlog items: *As a [user], I want [something], so that [benefit].* Add acceptance criteria.

**Cycle routine.** Plan the cycle (pick top backlog items), short check-ins, review what was delivered with stakeholders, and a retrospective on how to improve.

**AI review log.** Each time AI contributes to a plan, record what you asked, what it produced, what you verified and changed, and who approved the final version.

## Worked example

```
APPROACH DECISION: Karu Digital Skills Week
Chosen: Hybrid.
Predictive for: funding milestones, hall and generator bookings, trainer contracts,
  the report deadline (fixed by the bank, costly to change).
Agile for: session content. We'll run group A's session each week first, collect
  feedback, and adjust the content for group B and the following week.
Why: content needs are uncertain (learners' starting skills vary widely), but the
  logistics are fixed and contractual.
```

```
SESSION BACKLOG (ordered by value to learners)
# | User story                                                                          | Acceptance criteria
1 | As a learner, I want to spot a scam message, so that I don't lose money              | Can identify 4 of 5 sample scams in the practical check
2 | As a trader, I want to set up a WhatsApp Business catalogue, so customers see prices | Catalogue with 3 items created on own phone
3 | As a learner, I want to check a bank transaction safely, so I know I've been paid    | Can find and read a demo transaction alert
4 | As an older learner, I want larger text on my phone, so I can read messages          | Font size changed in settings
5 | As a learner, I want to make a video call, so I can talk to family                   | Completes a call with a partner in the room
```

```
AI REVIEW LOG
Date  | Output                  | Prompt (short)                    | Checked                               | Changed                                   | Approved by
3 Mar | Draft session backlog   | "user stories for digital skills" | Against learner registration survey   | Removed 3 stories needing laptops; reordered | Lead trainer
4 Mar | Approach comparison     | "predictive vs agile for..."      | Against bank contract terms            | Corrected: report date is fixed           | Chair
```

## Use AI well this week

```
Here is a summary of my project (pasted, no personal data). For each part of
the work, say whether a predictive or agile approach fits better and why,
using these factors: clarity of requirements, cost of change, availability of
feedback and fixed constraints. Then suggest a hybrid split.
```

> **Stay safe:** AI can't see your contracts or your stakeholders' expectations. Check any recommendation that affects a contractual or funder commitment with the sponsor before adopting it.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** Score your project on the four factors. Write your approach decision with reasons. Build a backlog of at least ten ordered user stories for the uncertain part of the work. Plan your first cycle. Start your AI review log.
