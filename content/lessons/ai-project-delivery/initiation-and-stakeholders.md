**Week 1 · About 15 to 20 hours · Competency 1: Outcomes and scope**

Most failing projects were in trouble on day one: nobody agreed what success looked like, who was in charge, or who needed to be involved. This week you'll start a project properly.

## What you'll be able to do

- Turn a need into a clear purpose and SMART objectives
- Write a project charter that authorises the project
- Identify stakeholders and analyse their interest, influence and concerns
- Plan how to engage each stakeholder

## Key ideas

**Need, outcome, output.** The need is the problem (adults in Karu can't safely use digital services). The outcome is the change (they can and do). Outputs are what the project delivers (classes, materials, a report).

**Project charter.** A short document that authorises the project. It names the sponsor (who funds and owns it), the coordinator and their authority, the purpose, objectives, high-level scope, key milestones, budget and major risks. Detailed planning comes later.

**Authority.** What the coordinator can decide alone, what needs the sponsor, and what needs a committee or funder. Writing this down early prevents arguments later.

**Stakeholders.** Anyone who affects or is affected by the project. Analyse each one's interest, influence, what they want, and what they're worried about.

| | Low interest | High interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High influence** | Keep satisfied | Manage closely |
| **Low influence** | Monitor | Keep informed |

## Worked example

```
PROJECT CHARTER: Karu Digital Skills Week (Phase 1)

Purpose: Help adults in Karu use smartphones safely for communication, business
and banking.
Objectives:
  1. 60 adults complete four 2-hour sessions by 30 March.
  2. At least 70% pass a practical check (send a WhatsApp Business catalogue,
     spot a scam message, check a transaction).
  3. Attendance and outcome report to the bank by 15 April.
Sponsor: Chair, Karu Community Association
Funder: [Bank] community programme, ₦1,800,000 (conditional on start by 10 March)
Coordinator: [Learner name], authority to spend up to ₦50,000 per item within the
  approved budget; anything above, or any scope change, goes to the sponsor.
In scope: training sessions, materials, venue, refreshments, report
Out of scope: buying laptops; follow-up classes (possible Phase 2)
Milestones: trainers confirmed (wk 3), registration open (wk 4), first class (wk 6),
  report (wk 12)
Key risks: unreliable power; low turnout; trainer availability
Approved by: Sponsor (name, date)  Funder contact (name, date)
```

```
STAKEHOLDER REGISTER (extract)
Stakeholder        | Interest | Influence | Wants                        | Concerns                      | Engagement
Association chair  | High     | High      | Visible success for the community | Budget, reputation        | Weekly meeting; approves changes
Bank programme lead| Medium   | High      | Start on time; credible report | Misuse of funds             | Fortnightly email; milestone reports
Learners           | High     | Low       | Useful, convenient sessions  | Cost, time, being embarrassed | Registration call; feedback each session
Trainers           | High     | Medium    | Clear brief, fair pay        | Late payment                  | Written agreement; weekly check-in
Hall caretaker     | Low      | Medium    | Hall left clean, on schedule | Damage, late finish           | Booking confirmation; reminder before each session
```

## Use AI well this week

AI is good at finding stakeholders and concerns you've missed.

```
I'm coordinating free digital skills classes for 60 adults at a community
hall in Nasarawa, funded by a bank. List possible stakeholders I might miss,
what each might want and fear, and one way to engage each. Mark which are
assumptions I should check.
```

Then check the list with the sponsor. AI doesn't know the local politics.

> **Stay safe:** Stakeholder registers can contain sensitive opinions about people. Keep them factual and professional, store them securely, and share only what each audience needs.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** For your partner project (or the Karu case), interview the sponsor for 20 minutes about purpose, success and authority. Draft the charter, then the stakeholder register. Ask the sponsor to correct both.

## Your evidence this week

Hand in, as described in the week 1 evidence brief:

1. A **project charter** with objectives, scope boundaries, authority and approval
2. A **stakeholder register** with interest, influence, wants, concerns and engagement
3. A short **note on authority**: what you can decide, and what you must escalate

What the assessor looks for: SMART objectives tied to the need, clear scope boundaries, explicit authority, and a realistic stakeholder analysis.

## Before you hand in

- [ ] Every objective is SMART
