**Week 8 · Independent competence assessment**

This week you show, on your own, that you can coordinate a project and get one back on track. An independent assessor gives you a project in trouble that you haven't seen before.

## How the assessment works

1. **The unseen recovery case.** A project mid-way through, with a charter, plan, logs and a set of problems: delays, overspend, a stakeholder conflict and a change request.
2. **Your response.** In the time given (usually one working day), you produce:
   - a **diagnosis** of what's gone wrong and why
   - a **recovery and delivery plan**: revised scope (if needed), schedule logic, budget and resources
   - an updated **risk register** and **communication plan**
   - a **status report** and **change analysis** with options and a recommendation
   - an **AI review log** for any AI-assisted outputs
3. **The oral defence.** About 30 minutes. You present your recovery plan; the assessor challenges your planning, cost and resource logic, risk responses and stakeholder decisions, and asks how you verified AI outputs.

## How you're judged

| Dimension | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Technical accuracy and completeness | 30% |
| Problem diagnosis and professional reasoning | 20% |
| Quality, usefulness and clarity of deliverable | 20% |
| Reproducibility, testing or measurement | 15% |
| Ethical practice, privacy and professional conduct | 10% |
| Oral defence and reflective improvement | 5% |

To be judged competent: **80% overall** and a pass on every mandatory requirement: honest status reporting; decisions beyond your authority escalated, not taken; no fabricated figures or progress; safety and safeguarding risks addressed; AI outputs verified.

> **If it didn't work:** If you're judged "not yet competent", you get written feedback on the specific gaps, a short remediation plan and a reassessment date.

## Practice case

Rehearse with this under timed conditions. It's fictional.

> **Market Stalls Digital Payments Rollout.** A traders' association in Suleja is helping 120 traders start accepting transfer payments, with ₦2,400,000 from a fintech partner. It's week 6 of 10. Only 35 traders are onboarded (plan: 72 by now). Two of four field agents have left. ₦1,500,000 is spent (plan: ₦1,300,000) because of extra transport. The association chair wants to add 40 traders from a neighbouring market "at no extra cost". The fintech partner wants a progress report on Friday.

Produce the deliverables above, then ask a fellow learner or your mentor to play the assessor.

## Prepare for the defence

Practise answering each of these in under two minutes:

- What's the real root cause of the delay? How do you know?
- Walk me through your revised schedule logic. What's on the critical path now?
- Where does the money come from for your recovery option?
- The chair insists on the 40 extra traders. What do you do, and who decides?
- What will you tell the funder on Friday, exactly?
- Show me one AI output you corrected. What was wrong?

## Assemble your graduate portfolio

| Item | From |
| --- | --- |
| Project charter | Week 1 |
| WBS | Week 2 |
| Schedule | Week 3 |
| Cost baseline | Week 3 |
| Risk register | Week 4 |
| Communications plan | Week 4 |
| Progress report | Weeks 6 and 7 |
| Change log | Week 7 |
| Lessons learned | Week 7 |

Remove every personal name (use roles), phone number and other personal detail. Use a code name for the partner organisation unless they've agreed in writing, and get permission before publishing any work sample.

> **Stay safe:** Describe your certificate as "demonstrated foundational competence in project coordination, independently assessed". Never imply equivalence to PMP, PRINCE2 or any other third-party credential, or to senior project-manager capability.

## After the pathway

Good next steps include project coordination roles, agile delivery, digital projects, development project management, and over time programme management and portfolio leadership. Keep your competence record going with each new piece of supervised real work.
