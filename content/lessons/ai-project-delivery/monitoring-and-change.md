**Week 6 · About 15 to 20 hours · Competencies 3 and 5: Control, report and escalate**

Plans meet reality. This week you'll learn to see early when a project drifts, report it honestly, handle changes formally, and offer the decision-maker clear options to recover.

## What you'll be able to do

- Compare actual progress and spend against baselines
- Write a status report with RAG ratings, evidence and asks
- Analyse change requests for their impact on scope, time, cost, quality and risk
- Develop and recommend recovery options, and escalate decisions beyond your authority

## Key ideas

**Baselines.** The approved scope, schedule and budget. You measure against them. They change only through change control.

**RAG status.** Green: on track. Amber: at risk, but recoverable within your authority. Red: off track; needs a decision above you. Each rating needs a reason.

**Simple progress measures.** Milestones due versus done; spend to date versus planned spend to date; deliverables accepted versus planned.

**Issue log.** Current problems, their impact, owner, action and due date.

**Change control.** Any change to baselined scope, schedule or budget follows the same steps: log the request → analyse the impact → recommend → the right authority decides → update the plan and tell everyone affected.

**Escalation.** Raise a decision when it's beyond your authority, a tolerance is breached, or a risk becomes serious. Escalate early, with analysis, options and a recommendation, not just the problem.

## Worked example

```
STATUS REPORT: Week 7 of 12          Overall: AMBER
Schedule: AMBER: sessions on plan, but group B's session 2 was cancelled (generator failure).
Budget:   GREEN: spent ₦842,000 of ₦910,000 planned to date.
Scope:    GREEN: no approved changes.
Quality:  GREEN: observer checklists 100% complete; learner feedback average 4.4/5 (n = 51).
Risks:    R1 (power) has occurred → now issue I-03.

Milestones: trainers confirmed ✓; registration ✓ (64 registered); sessions 1–2 group A ✓;
            session 2 group B ✗ (rescheduled, see I-03).

Issues
I-03 | Generator failed 40 min into group B session 2 | 29 learners missed practical | Coordinator | Hire firm replacing unit; catch-up proposed | 14 Mar

Decision needed from the chair by Friday: approve the catch-up option below (CR-02).
```

```
CHANGE REQUEST CR-02
Request: Add a catch-up session for group B (raised by lead trainer, 11 Mar)
Options:
  A. Saturday catch-up at the hall: +₦62,000 (hall, generator, fuel, trainers,
     refreshments), no change to end date. Funded from contingency (₦145,200 left).
  B. Merge the missed content into session 3: no cost; session 3 overruns by 45 min
     or practical time drops; quality risk.
  C. Do nothing: 29 learners miss the scam-awareness practical; objective 2 at risk.
Recommendation: A. Protects objective 2 and stays within budget.
Authority: above coordinator limit (₦50,000) → chair decides; bank informed in
  next fortnightly update as a use of contingency.
Decision: ______  Decided by: ______  Date: ______
```

## Use AI well this week

AI can help turn notes into a clear status report and challenge your options. It must not soften the facts.

```
Turn these notes into a one-page status report with RAG ratings (pasted, no
personal data). Keep every figure exactly as given. Don't change the overall
status. Then list any option I've missed for recovering the cancelled session.
```

> **Stay safe:** Never change a RAG rating, delay bad news, or adjust figures to make a report look better. Honest reporting and escalation are mandatory requirements.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** Your mentor will give you a disruption scenario for your project (for example, a trainer leaves, the funder delays payment, or the sponsor asks for extra scope). Write the status report, log the issue, analyse the change request with three options, and draft the escalation message.
