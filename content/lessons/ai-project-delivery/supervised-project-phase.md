**Week 7 · About 15 to 20 hours · All five competencies**

This week you coordinate a real phase of a small community digital-literacy or business improvement project, under your mentor's supervision. Your mentor will also introduce scenario-based disruptions to test how you handle change.

## What you'll be able to do

- Run the coordination routine for a live project phase
- Keep decision, issue and change logs that show authority and reasoning
- Communicate with stakeholders according to your plan
- Respond to disruptions through issue management and change control
- Capture lessons learned

## Key ideas

**Supervised.** You coordinate; the sponsor decides within their authority; your mentor reviews your logs and decisions daily. You don't take on decisions beyond your agreed authority.

**Coordination routine.** A predictable rhythm: a short daily check-in, updates to the logs, communications sent as planned, and a weekly status report.

**Logs as evidence.** Your decision log, issue log and change log are the record of how you coordinated. Keep them current; they're the core of your evidence this week.

**Scenario disruptions.** During the week your mentor will inject at least one disruption, such as a key person unavailable, a funding delay or a new request from a stakeholder. Handle it exactly as you would a real one.

**Lessons learned.** What worked, what didn't, and what you'd do differently, written so the next project can use them.

## Logs

```
DECISION LOG
#  | Date | Decision | Options considered | Decided by (authority) | Reason | Impact / follow-up

ISSUE LOG
#  | Date raised | Issue | Impact | Owner | Action | Due | Status

CHANGE LOG
#  | Date | Change requested | By | Impact (scope/time/cost/quality/risk) | Decision | Decided by | Plan updated?

LESSONS LEARNED
#  | What happened | Effect | What we'd repeat or change | Who should know
```

## Running the week

| Day | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Confirm the phase scope, your authority and the routine with the sponsor and mentor |
| 2 | Coordinate delivery; update logs; send planned communications |
| 3 | Respond to the injected disruption: issue, change analysis, escalation |
| 4 | Mentor critique of your logs and decisions; adjust |
| 5 | Status report; lessons-learned session; handover of the phase |

## Use AI well this week

AI can help you draft communications, summarise check-ins and organise lessons learned. Keep the review habit: every AI-drafted message is checked for accuracy and tone, and approved where needed, before it's sent.

```
Draft a short WhatsApp update for learners (opted in) from these facts:
[date, time, venue change, what to bring]. Plain English and Hausa versions,
under 60 words each. Don't add any information that isn't in the facts.
```

> **Stay safe:** Collect only the personal data the project needs, keep it secure, and never use learners' contact details for anything they didn't agree to. Safeguarding concerns go straight to the sponsor.

## Your evidence this week

Hand in, as described in the week 7 evidence brief:

1. **Evidence of coordination**: your daily check-in notes and communications sent (with personal data removed)
2. Your **decision log** and **issue log**, kept current through the week
3. Your **change log**, including how you handled the injected disruption
4. A weekly **status report** and **lessons learned**

What the assessor looks for: steady coordination, decisions made at the right level of authority, issues and changes handled formally, honest reporting, and useful lessons.

## Before you hand in

- [ ] Every decision in my log names who decided and on what authority
- [ ] Every issue has an owner, action and due date
- [ ] The disruption went through issue management and, if needed, change control
- [ ] My status report is honest and backed by evidence
- [ ] No personal data appears in my evidence
