**Week 4 · About 15 to 20 hours · Competency 3: Control**

This week you plan for what could go wrong, what "good" looks like, and how everyone will stay informed. These three plans are what keep a project steady when things change.

## What you'll be able to do

- Identify risks and score their likelihood and impact
- Choose risk responses and assign owners
- Define quality criteria and how they'll be checked
- Write a communication plan based on your stakeholder register

## Key ideas

**Risk statement.** Write risks as cause, event and effect: "Because the hall's power is unreliable, a session may lose power, causing learners to miss practical work."

**Scoring.** Likelihood and impact on a 1 to 5 scale; multiply for a score. Focus on the highest.

**Responses.**

| Response | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Avoid | Change the plan to remove the risk | Hold sessions in daylight only |
| Reduce | Lower likelihood or impact | Test generator; carry spare fuel |
| Transfer | Shift it to someone better placed | Hire contract makes the firm replace a faulty generator |
| Accept | Take it, with a fallback | Low turnout in week 1: accept and do more outreach |

**Risk owner.** One named person who watches the risk and acts. The coordinator doesn't own every risk.

**Quality.** Quality criteria say what "good" means for each deliverable; quality checks say how and when you'll test it. Quality is planned in, not inspected at the end.

**Communication plan.** For each stakeholder: what they need, how often, which channel, from whom, and what they're asked to do.

## Use AI well this week

AI is useful for generating risks you haven't thought of. You decide which are real.

```
I'm coordinating 8 digital skills sessions for 60 adults at a community hall
in Nasarawa (summary pasted). List 15 risks written as cause → event → effect,
grouped by people, venue, money, safety and reputation. Don't score them.
```

> **Stay safe:** Safeguarding and financial-harm risks always get a response, however low their score. Learners must never enter real PINs or passwords during training.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** Run a 30-minute risk workshop with your sponsor or team. Score and respond to the top ten risks, with owners. Write quality criteria for your main deliverables, then the communication plan from your stakeholder register.

## Your evidence this week

Hand in, as described in the week 4 evidence brief:

1. A **risk register** with cause-event-effect statements, scores, responses, owners and triggers
2. **Quality criteria** and checks for your main deliverables
3. A **communication plan** for every stakeholder in your register

What the assessor looks for: well-written risks with practical responses and owners, quality planned in, and communication matched to each stakeholder.
