**Week 8 · Independent competence assessment**

This week you show, on your own, that you can turn an unfamiliar programme dataset into reliable evidence. An independent assessor gives you the data and scores your work.

## How the assessment works

1. **The unseen dataset.** A programme summary, results framework and raw dataset you haven't seen before, with a stakeholder's questions.
2. **Your analysis.** In the time given (usually one working day), you produce:
   - a **cleaning log** and clean dataset, with reproducible steps
   - an **indicator workbook**
   - a short **report** (2 to 4 pages) answering the stakeholder's questions, with a limitations statement
   - a **dashboard** (1 to 2 pages)
3. **The oral defence.** About 30 minutes. The assessor asks you to explain your choices, challenges your interpretation, and may ask you to recalculate or reword something.

You may use your notes, past work and an approved AI assistant, but never with personal data. Log your AI use.

## How you're judged

This pathway uses its own weights:

| Dimension | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Programme logic and indicators | 15% |
| Data quality and reproducibility | 20% |
| Accuracy and interpretation | 25% |
| Reporting | 15% |
| Judgment | 15% |
| Oral defence | 10% |

To be judged competent: **80% overall** and a pass on the mandatory requirements of **integrity** (no invented, altered or selectively reported findings; every figure traceable) and **privacy** (no personal data exposed in outputs or to unapproved AI services).

> **If it didn't work:** If you're judged "not yet competent", you get written feedback on the specific gaps, a short remediation plan and a reassessment date.

## Practice challenge

Rehearse with this under timed conditions. Your mentor will share the practice files.

> **SkillUp Digital Girls** (fictional) trained 320 young women aged 16 to 24 in Kaduna and Kano in basic digital skills over 6 months. The dataset has intake and 3-month follow-up records, including duplicates, missing follow-ups and inconsistent location spellings. The programme officer asks: How many completed? What share report using digital skills to earn or study at 3 months? Who is the project missing? What can we honestly tell the donor?

## Prepare for the oral defence

Practise answering each of these in under two minutes:

- Talk me through your denominator for this indicator. Why that one?
- What did you do about missing follow-ups? How might it bias your result?
- Show me how I'd reproduce your clean dataset from the raw file.
- This finding: is it an output, an outcome, or a causal claim? How do you know?
- Where did you use AI? Show me how you checked it.
- What one piece of extra data would most improve this analysis?

## Assemble your graduate portfolio

| Item | From |
| --- | --- |
| Theory of Change | Week 1 |
| Kobo form | Week 2 |
| Data dictionary (codebook) | Week 2 |
| Cleaning script or workbook | Week 3 |
| Indicator tables | Week 4 |
| Dashboard | Week 5 |
| Analytical report | Week 7 |
| Limitations statement | Week 7 |

Use only fictional or properly anonymised data in your portfolio. Remove every name, phone number, exact location and participant ID that could identify someone, and get permission before publishing anything from a real organisation.

> **Stay safe:** Describe your certificate as "demonstrated foundational competence, independently assessed". Don't claim to be an evaluator or statistician, or to hold any professional accreditation you don't hold.

## After the pathway

Good next steps include impact evaluation, applied statistics, humanitarian analytics, research, GIS and social protection data systems. Keep your competence record going with each new piece of supervised real work.
