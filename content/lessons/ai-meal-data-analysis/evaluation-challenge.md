**Week 7 · About 15 to 20 hours · All five competencies**

This week you apply everything to a realistic NGO scenario: a fictional or properly anonymised youth livelihoods or women's empowerment project. You'll produce the full analysis package an NGO would need for a performance review.

## What you'll be able to do

- Scope an analysis with an NGO stakeholder's questions in mind
- Run the full workflow: indicators, data quality, analysis, visualisation, reporting
- Distinguish outputs, outcomes and causal claims throughout
- Write a clear limitations statement and practical recommendations

## The scenario

Your mentor will give you one of two scenario packs (fictional or anonymised). Each contains a project summary, a results framework, a baseline and endline dataset, open-ended feedback, and a request from the programme manager. For example:

> **Women Thrive Savings Project.** A fictional NGO formed 40 village savings and loan groups for 900 women in Nasarawa and Niger states over 18 months, with business skills training. The donor's mid-term review asks: Is the project reaching the intended women? Are members saving and growing businesses? Do women report more say in household decisions? What should change in the final year?

## Key ideas

**Answer the questions asked.** Structure the analysis around the stakeholder's questions. Every table and chart should help answer one.

**Three levels of claim.**

| Level | Example | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | 38 of 40 groups meeting monthly | Project records |
| Outcome (descriptive) | 72% of members have savings at endline, up from 18% at baseline | Baseline and endline data, consistent definitions |
| Causal | The project increased savings by X points | A suitable design: comparison group, randomisation or a credible quasi-experimental method |

**Limitations statement.** Say what limits the data (self-report, non-response, small groups), what limits the design (no comparison group), how this might affect the findings (direction of possible bias), and what would strengthen future evidence.

**Recommendations.** Practical, linked to findings, owned by someone, and realistic for the project's remaining time and budget.

## The analysis package

```
ANALYSIS PACKAGE CONTENTS
1. Summary (1 page): the questions, key findings, main limitations, recommendations
2. Results framework with each indicator's result, n, baseline and target
3. Data quality note and cleaning log
4. Indicator workbook (formulas visible)
5. Dashboard (2–3 pages) with definitions page
6. Qualitative findings with codebook and validation
7. Limitations statement
8. Recommendations (with owner and timing)
9. AI-use log
```

## Use AI well this week

Use AI to challenge your conclusions before a stakeholder does.

```
Here are my key findings and the evaluation design (pasted, no personal data).
Act as a sceptical donor reviewer. For each finding, say whether the evidence
supports it, what alternative explanations exist, and how I should reword it
if it overclaims.
```

> **Stay safe:** If a stakeholder asks you to overstate results, explain the risk to the organisation's credibility and offer accurate wording. Record the request and your response.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** Day 1: read the scenario pack, list the stakeholder's questions and plan your analysis. Days 2 to 3: clean, calculate and build the dashboard. Day 4: code qualitative data, draft findings, mentor critique. Day 5: revise, write limitations and recommendations.

## Your evidence this week

Hand in, as described in the week 7 evidence brief:

What the assessor looks for: accuracy, reproducibility, findings that answer the questions asked, clear separation of outputs, outcomes and causal claims, and an honest limitations statement.
