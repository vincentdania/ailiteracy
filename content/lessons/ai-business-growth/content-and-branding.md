**Week 3 · About 15 to 20 hours · Competency 3: Content**

This week you'll create the content: the words and images a customer actually sees. You'll use AI to draft and vary it, and you'll be the editor who makes sure it's true, clear and usable by everyone.

## What you'll be able to do

- Write copy for a specific segment and a single action
- Edit AI drafts for accuracy, tone and local fit
- Produce simple, accessible social assets in Canva or Figma
- Explain your creative choices in a short rationale

## Key ideas

**One audience, one action.** Every piece of content should be for one segment and ask for one thing: "Request your meeting tray quote on WhatsApp", not five calls to action at once.

**The message hierarchy.** What the customer must see first, second and third. For an office buyer: what it is and the price; how fast and reliable it is; how to order and get an invoice.

**Brand consistency.** The same name, colours, tone and promises everywhere a customer meets the business. If Instagram says 24 hours' notice, WhatsApp shouldn't say 48.

**Accessible content.** Text that's large and high-contrast, key details in the caption as well as the image, alt text for images, and plain language.

**Truthful claims.** Only say what the business can show: real prices, real delivery times, real reviews with permission. No invented awards, statistics or testimonials.

## Worked example

Before and after, for an office tray post:

```
BEFORE (owner's post)
"Yummy small chops!!! 😋😋 DM for price. #abujafood #smallchops"

AFTER
Headline (on image): Meeting trays from ₦45,000 · Delivered in Wuse 2 & Maitama
Caption: Ordering for a meeting? Our trays serve 15 to 20 people. Order by
2pm for next-day delivery. We send a company invoice before you pay.
Request a quote on WhatsApp: tap the link in our bio.
Alt text: A tray of small chops (puff-puff, samosas, spring rolls) on a
boardroom table.

RATIONALE
- Segment: office administrators. Research showed they need price, notice
  time and an invoice before they can order (interviews 2, 3, 5).
- Price is shown because competitors hide it and two interviewees said they
  skip businesses that "DM for price".
- One action only: request a quote on WhatsApp, where orders are handled.
- The invoice line answers the most common objection.
```

## Use AI well this week

AI is useful for generating variations quickly. Give it the facts, the audience and the rules, and ask for options, not a final answer.

```
Write 5 short Instagram captions for a bakery's office meeting trays.
Facts (use only these): trays from ₦45,000, serve 15 to 20 people, order
by 2pm for next-day delivery in Wuse 2 and Maitama, company invoice available.
Audience: office administrators. One call to action: request a quote on
WhatsApp. No invented claims, no statistics, no testimonials, under 60 words.
```

Then edit the best one. Check every fact against the business's own information, and read it aloud to check it sounds like the business, not like a generic AI.

> **Stay safe:** Don't use photos of customers, staff or other people's work without permission. Don't use AI image tools to create fake "customer" photos or reviews.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** Take three existing posts or messages from your partner business. Rewrite each for one segment and one action. Create one graphic in Canva or Figma. Check contrast with a free contrast checker and write alt text.

## Your evidence this week

Hand in, as described in the week 3 evidence brief:

1. **Revised copy**: at least three pieces, each shown before and after
2. **Social assets**: at least two graphics or short videos, with alt text
3. A **creative rationale** linking each choice to your week 2 research
4. A **claims check** listing every factual claim and its source

What the assessor looks for: accuracy, a clear audience and action, accessibility, and choices you can justify from evidence.
