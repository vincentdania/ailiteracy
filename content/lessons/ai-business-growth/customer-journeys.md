**Week 1 · About 15 to 20 hours · Competency 1: Customers and goals**

Marketing starts with customers, not with posts or ads. This week you'll learn a small set of fundamentals and use them to map how one type of customer finds, chooses and returns to a business. You'll also set the AI habits you'll use for the rest of the pathway.

## What you'll be able to do

- Explain the difference between a business goal, a marketing objective and a metric
- Map a customer journey from awareness to loyalty, using evidence
- Spot where customers drop out, and suggest why
- Use an AI assistant to draft and challenge your thinking, then verify and log what it gives you

## Key ideas

**Goal, objective, metric.** A business goal is what the owner wants (more office orders). A marketing objective is the change in customer behaviour that gets there (more office managers request a quote). A metric is how you'll know (quote requests per week).

**Segments.** Different customers buy for different reasons. A birthday cake buyer and an office administrator ordering a meeting tray are different segments, even if they buy similar things.

**The customer journey.** Most purchases move through five stages:

| Stage | The customer's question | Example for Zuma Bakes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Who can help with this? | An admin officer needs snacks for Friday's board meeting |
| Consideration | Which option is best for me? | Compares two bakeries' menus, prices and delivery times |
| Purchase | How do I order, and can I trust them? | Sends a WhatsApp message, waits for a reply and an invoice |
| Experience | Did it go as promised? | Tray arrives on time, looks as pictured |
| Loyalty and advocacy | Will I use them again, and tell others? | Saves the number, recommends to the HR team |

**Touchpoints and friction.** A touchpoint is anywhere the customer meets the business: a post, a WhatsApp reply, a delivery rider. Friction is anything that slows or stops them: no prices shown, slow replies, no company invoice.

> **New word: conversion.** When a customer takes the step you wanted, such as sending an order. The **conversion rate** is how many took the step out of how many could have.

## Worked example

The Zuma Bakes owner says offices "never order". Here's a short journey for the office segment, with evidence marked.

```
SEGMENT: Office administrators in Wuse 2 and Maitama ordering for meetings

AWARENESS
  Touchpoints: colleague recommendation; Google search "small chops Wuse"
  Evidence: 2 current office customers both came by referral (owner)
  Gap: does the bakery appear in Google Maps results? (check)

CONSIDERATION
  Touchpoints: Instagram, WhatsApp catalogue
  Friction: no tray prices or minimum notice shown anywhere (checked Instagram)

PURCHASE
  Touchpoints: WhatsApp chat, bank transfer
  Friction: offices often need a company invoice before payment (ask the 2 customers)

EXPERIENCE
  Evidence: both office customers reordered (owner)

LOYALTY
  Gap: no follow-up after delivery; no way to reorder quickly
```

The map already suggests the problem is less about awareness than about consideration and purchase: offices can't see prices or get an invoice easily.

## Use AI well this week

AI is good at generating questions and options you hadn't thought of. It's poor at knowing facts about a specific local business. Use it to widen your thinking, then check.

Try this prompt with your AI assistant:

```
I'm mapping the customer journey for office administrators who order meeting
snacks from a small bakery in Abuja. Do not invent statistics.

1. List the questions this customer is likely to have at each stage:
   awareness, consideration, purchase, experience, loyalty.
2. For each stage, list three possible points of friction.
3. Mark which of your points are assumptions I should test with real customers.
```

Then do the responsible-AI exercise below.

> **Stay safe:** Never paste customer names, phone numbers or chat screenshots into an AI tool. Describe the situation in general terms instead.

## Responsible-AI practice exercise

Ask your AI assistant: *"Give me five statistics about how Nigerian businesses order catering, with sources."*

For each statistic it returns:

1. Try to find the original source.
2. Record whether it exists, says what the AI claimed, and is recent enough to use.
3. Write one sentence on what this tells you about using AI in client work.

```
AI CLAIM CHECK
Claim | Source the AI gave | Found? (yes / no / different) | Usable? | Note
```

Most learners find at least one claim that can't be traced. That's the point of the exercise.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** Pick one segment of your partner business (or Zuma Bakes if you don't have one yet). Talk to at least two real customers, or the owner, for ten minutes each. Ask how they first heard of the business, what nearly stopped them buying, and what would make them come back. Then draft the five-stage journey.

## Your evidence this week

Hand in, as described in the week 1 evidence brief:

1. A **customer journey map** for one segment, with touchpoints, friction and evidence for each stage, and gaps clearly marked
2. A **measurable goal** for the business, with the objective and metric that support it
3. Your **responsible-AI exercise** table and one-paragraph reflection

What the assessor looks for: evidence over assumption, friction points that are specific enough to act on, and honest marking of what you don't yet know.
