**Week 2 · About 15 to 20 hours · Competency 2: Research and channels**

Good marketing decisions rest on evidence. This week you'll gather just enough of it: what competitors offer, who the customers really are, and where they spend their attention.

## What you'll be able to do

- Plan research that fits the size of the decision and the budget
- Run a competitor scan and turn it into useful comparisons
- Define two or three customer segments that differ in ways that matter for marketing
- Keep an evidence log so every claim in your work can be traced

## Key ideas

**Primary and secondary research.** Primary research is what you collect yourself: interviews, a short survey, watching how people order. Secondary research already exists: competitor pages, reviews, published reports, the business's own sales records.

**Proportionate research.** For a small business, a sensible week of research might be five customer conversations, a scan of five competitors, and a look at three months of orders. That's enough to make a good first decision and test it.

**Segments that matter.** A useful segment differs in what it needs, how it decides or where you can reach it. "Women aged 25 to 40" is a demographic; "office admins who must get an invoice approved before paying" is a segment you can market to.

**Evidence strength.** Not all evidence is equal. Rate it:

| Strength | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Strong | The business's own order records; several customers saying the same thing unprompted |
| Medium | Competitor prices you checked yourself; a published report from a named organisation |
| Weak | One person's opinion; an unsourced statistic; an AI summary you haven't checked |

## Worked example

A competitor scan for Zuma Bakes' office trays:

```
COMPETITOR SCAN  (checked 3 to 5 November)
Competitor | Offer | Tray price | Notice needed | Invoice? | Where they show up | Reviews (themes)
Bakery A   | Meeting packs | ₦38,000–₦60,000 | 24 hours | Yes, on letterhead | Google Maps, LinkedIn | "Always on time"
Bakery B   | Small chops only | ₦35,000 | 48 hours | Not stated | Instagram | "Tasty, slow replies"
Caterer C  | Full catering | From ₦150,000 | 1 week | Yes | Website, referrals | "Pricey, reliable"

WHAT THIS SUGGESTS
- Offices value punctuality and invoices more than price alone.
- Bakery A appears where office buyers search (Google Maps, LinkedIn); Zuma Bakes doesn't.
- No one offers a simple "same-day tray" option: a possible gap to test.
```

## Use AI well this week

AI can speed up research planning and help you organise what you find. Use it to draft interview questions and to sort notes into themes, then check it against the originals.

```
I'm researching why offices in Abuja choose a bakery for meeting snacks.
Draft 8 open interview questions for an office administrator. Avoid leading
questions. Then suggest how I could group the answers into themes.
```

When you ask AI to summarise reviews or interview notes, paste in text with names and contact details removed, then read at least a third of the originals to check the themes are fair.

> **Stay safe:** Ask permission before interviewing anyone, tell them how their answers will be used, and don't record names next to answers in your notes.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** Scan five competitors for your partner business using the table above. Then run at least three customer conversations of 10 to 15 minutes. Write each conversation up as notes with no names, then group the notes into themes.
