**Week 5 · About 15 to 20 hours · Competency 4: Campaigns**

This week you set up the campaign, under supervision. You'll configure it in an ad-platform sandbox or in the business's own account with the owner's approval, capture leads with the minimum personal data, and build a tracking plan so results can be measured honestly.

## What you'll be able to do

- Configure a campaign's objective, audience, budget, schedule and creative
- Set up lead capture that collects only what's needed, with consent
- Tag links and label leads so every result can be traced to its source
- Run a pre-launch check with the owner and record their approval

## Key ideas

**Campaign structure.** Most ad platforms use three levels: the campaign (objective and budget), the ad set or ad group (audience, placement, schedule) and the ad (the creative). Keep tests simple: change one thing at a time.

**Audience targeting.** Location, interests and behaviours. Narrow is not always better: very small audiences can be expensive and slow to learn. Never target by sensitive traits such as health, religion or ethnicity.

**Lead capture.** Where an interested person lands: a WhatsApp chat with a pre-filled message, a short form, a phone call. Ask only for what you need to follow up, and say how the details will be used.

**Tracking plan.** A short document that says how each KPI will be counted, where, and by whom. Include UTM tags for links and a labelling rule for leads that arrive by message.

> **New word: UTM tag.** Extra text on the end of a link, such as `?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign=office_trays_feb`, that tells analytics where a visit came from.

## Worked example

```
TRACKING PLAN: Zuma Bakes office trays

KPI                       | Where counted             | How                                   | Who, when
Quote requests (primary)  | WhatsApp Business labels  | Staff add label "Office-Feb" + source  | Shop manager, daily
Ad clicks to WhatsApp     | Instagram Ads Manager     | Click-to-WhatsApp objective           | Learner, Mon & Thu
Profile visits from Maps  | Google Business Profile   | Performance tab                       | Learner, weekly
Confirmed orders, value   | Order book (Sheet)        | Column "Source" filled at order time  | Owner, at each order

Links
Bio link: wa.me/... ?text=Hi%2C%20I%27d%20like%20a%20meeting%20tray%20quote
Menu PDF link: zumabakes.example/trays?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=office_trays_feb

Source question: staff ask every new office customer "How did you hear about us?"
and record the answer in the order book.
```

## Pre-launch check

Go through this with the owner before anything goes live, and keep a copy of their approval:

```
PRE-LAUNCH CHECK
[ ] Objective matches the plan
[ ] Location and audience are as agreed; no sensitive targeting
[ ] Daily budget: ₦____  Total: ₦____  End date: ____
[ ] Stop rule recorded: ____
[ ] Creative and captions match the approved versions; all claims true
[ ] Lead capture asks only for: ____
[ ] Tracking plan in place; links tested on a phone
[ ] Account owner: the business. My access level: ____
[ ] Owner's approval (name, date, message or signature): ____
```

AI can help you draft ad variations and check your tracking plan for gaps.


> **Stay safe:** If you use a live account, work only with the access the owner gave you. Never add your own payment card or change the business's billing details. Use the platform's sandbox or preview tools wherever you can.

## Guided practice

> **Try it:** Set up the campaign in a sandbox or as a draft in the business's account. Tag every link and test each one on a phone. Walk the owner through the pre-launch check and record their approval before anything goes live.

## Your evidence this week

Hand in, as described in the week 5 evidence brief:

1. **Campaign configuration**: screenshots or an export of the settings, with any account IDs and personal details hidden
2. A **tracking plan** covering every KPI
3. The completed **pre-launch check** with the owner's approval
4. Your **lead capture** design and what personal data it collects, and why

What the assessor looks for: settings that match the approved plan, minimal personal data, tracking that would really work, and clear client control.
