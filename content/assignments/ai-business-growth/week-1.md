## Evidence: Customer journey map and responsible-AI practice

Map how one segment of your partner business (or Zuma Bakes) moves from awareness to loyalty, based on conversations with real customers or the owner. Then test an AI tool's statistics against their sources.

### What to submit

1. **Your customer journey map.** One segment, five stages, with touchpoints, friction and the evidence for each stage. Mark every gap you haven't confirmed.
2. **Your measurable goal.** The business goal, the marketing objective that supports it and the metric you'd use, with a number, a starting point and a date.
3. **Your AI claim check.** The table of five AI-supplied statistics: the source given, whether you found it, and whether it's usable.
4. **Your reflection.** One paragraph on what the claim check tells you about using AI in client work.

### What the assessor looks for

- Evidence over assumption, with gaps honestly marked
- Friction points specific enough to act on
- A goal that can be checked later
- A careful, honest AI claim check

## Revision: Act on your feedback

Your mentor will return written feedback on your evidence against the rubric. Revise your work, then explain what you changed. This is where most of the learning happens, so treat it as part of the week, not an extra.

### What to submit

1. **Your revised evidence.** The improved version, or a link to it, with the changed parts marked.
2. **What you changed.** Each feedback point, what you changed in response, and anything you chose not to change and why.
3. **AI-use log.** The rows you added this week: what you asked, what you kept, what you changed and how you checked it.
