## Commercial verdict
The revised course is a stronger practical introductory product, not proof of mass demand. Five million paying Nigerians is an unvalidated ambition. The strongest value is reusable work outputs, Nigerian workplace framing and verification habits. Generic prompts alone do not justify the price.

Before broad promotion, test with paying learners from distinct segments. Measure completion, first useful output, repeat use, corrections needed, willingness to recommend and refund reasons. Use actual learner feedback and permissioned before/after examples rather than invented success stories.

Suggested pilot: recruit a small mixed cohort and observe them completing tasks on their own phones. This is a recommendation, not a completed trial. Decide acceptance thresholds before the pilot. Consider sponsored/employer-funded access and instalment options only after examining costs and payment operations; do not assume ₦20,000 is affordable across the mass market.

## Remaining gaps and release checks
- Examples added are illustrative, not documented real-world case studies. Obtain consented cases from learners.
- Verify current product feature availability and signup instructions against official documentation before release.
- Confirm the claimed instructor recording is actually linked and playable; otherwise remove the promise.
- Check certificate behaviour in the app against the revised Day 7 and Day 10 copy.
- Conduct phone-screen review and timed learner testing; added material increases reading time.
- Some original rhetoric and time estimates remain. A teaching pilot must establish achievable durations; no universal speed or earnings claims should be made.
- Manual prompt workflows must not be marketed as autonomous agents.

## Recommendation
Proceed to a controlled paid pilot after the release checks. Do not market the course as commercially validated or capable of delivering guaranteed outcomes to five million buyers.
