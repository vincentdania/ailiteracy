# Assessor guide: AI Business Growth Specialist

**Confidential. For mentors and independent assessors only. Do not seed, publish or share with learners.**

## The decision

You decide whether the learner has **demonstrated foundational competence** on an unfamiliar task. You are not judging effort, attendance or improvement. Record **competent** or **not yet competent**, with reasons.

Competent requires **80% overall** and a pass on every mandatory requirement.

## Assessment conditions

- One working day (about 7 hours) for the written deliverables, then a 30-minute oral defence on the same or next day.
- Open book: the learner may use notes, past work and an approved AI assistant. They must log AI use.
- The brief must be new to the learner. Rotate briefs between cohorts; never reuse a brief a learner has seen.
- The independent assessor must not have mentored the learner.

## Unseen brief A

> **Nnamdi Auto Care** is a car wash and detailing service in Lugbe, Abuja, with 6 staff. Weekday mornings are quiet; Saturdays have queues. The owner wants more weekday customers and has heard about "WhatsApp marketing". Budget: up to ₦80,000 over six weeks. Known: standard wash ₦3,500; full detailing ₦25,000; about 40 cars on a weekday and 140 on a Saturday; 600 customer phone numbers saved in the shop phone, collected over three years with no record of consent. Unknown: repeat rate, margins, which customers are local residents versus passing traffic.
>
> Produce: a growth plan, two pieces of copy and one visual, a measurement approach and a risk register.

**Traps to watch:** the 600 numbers without consent (a learner who plans to bulk-message them fails authorization/privacy unless they propose an opt-in step first); measuring "weekday customers" needs a baseline the shop can count; demand shifting from Saturday to weekdays is not new revenue.

## Unseen brief B

> **Bright Steps Tutors** offers after-school maths and English tutoring for primary pupils in Kubwa, Abuja, from a rented classroom. 22 pupils are enrolled; capacity is 40. Parents find them through church announcements and word of mouth. The director wants 12 more pupils before the next term starts in 8 weeks. Budget: ₦120,000. Known: fee ₦25,000 per month; 3 parents left last term citing "no progress reports". Unknown: local competitors' fees, how many enquiries become enrolments.
>
> Produce: a growth plan, two pieces of copy and one visual, a measurement approach and a risk register.

**Traps to watch:** claims about exam results or guaranteed improvement (fabrication unless sourced); the retention problem (progress reports) may matter more than acquisition; marketing to children directly; photos of pupils without parental consent.

## Scoring

Score each dimension on the four levels below, then convert: Not yet = 25% of the weight, Developing = 55%, Competent = 85%, Strong = 100%.

| Dimension (weight) | Not yet | Developing | Competent | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical accuracy and completeness (30) | Deliverables missing or wrong calculations | All present; some errors or vague KPIs | Complete; SMART objectives; correct metrics; budget adds up | As competent, plus well-chosen secondary KPIs and realistic phasing |
| Problem diagnosis and reasoning (20) | Jumps to tactics | Identifies the problem but channel choices weakly justified | Diagnosis from the brief's evidence; channels justified; assumptions named | Spots the non-obvious issue (e.g. retention, consent) and reshapes the plan |
| Quality and clarity of deliverable (20) | Unclear or unusable | Usable with heavy editing | Owner could act on it; creatives accurate, targeted and accessible | Polished, concise, persuasive and accessible |
| Measurement (15) | No measurement plan | Metrics listed but not countable | Baseline, counting method and review points; attribution limits stated | Includes a simple test (e.g. holdout week or coded offer) |
| Ethics, privacy and conduct (10) | — (see mandatory) | Minor gaps (e.g. no alt text) | Consent and client control explicit; claims sourced | Proactively protects customers and the business |
| Oral defence and reflection (5) | Defensive or can't explain | Explains but struggles to revise | Defends with evidence; revises sensibly when challenged | Revises quickly and improves the plan |

## Mandatory requirements (any fail = not yet competent)

- [ ] **Ethics:** no fabricated claims, statistics, awards or testimonials
- [ ] **Data integrity:** every figure from the brief or marked as an assumption; calculations reproducible
- [ ] **Authorization:** no spend, messaging or account changes without the owner's approval; no messaging of contacts without consent
- [ ] **Safety and privacy:** no personal data in AI tools; no sensitive targeting; nothing aimed at children directly

## Oral defence questions

Pick four or five. Push on the weakest part of the plan.

1. Walk me through why this is your main channel. What evidence in the brief supports it?
2. Which number are you least confident about? What would you do if it's half what you assumed?
3. Here's new information: [the owner says the budget is now half]. Revise the plan now.
4. How will you know whether the campaign caused any increase?
5. What could go wrong for customers, not just the business?
6. Show me your AI-use log. Which output did you reject, and why?

Record the learner's answers and any revision they make.

## Moderation

A second assessor reviews every result between 75% and 85%, every mandatory fail, and a random 20% of the rest. Disagreements are discussed and the agreed decision recorded with both names. Track assessor agreement across cohorts.
