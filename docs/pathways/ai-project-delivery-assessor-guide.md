# Assessor guide: AI-Enabled Project Delivery Practitioner

**Confidential. For mentors and independent assessors only. Do not seed, publish or share with learners.**

## The decision

You decide whether the learner has **demonstrated foundational competence in project coordination** on an unseen recovery case. This is not a judgement of senior project-manager capability. Record **competent** or **not yet competent**, with reasons. Competent requires **80% overall** and a pass on every mandatory requirement.

## Assessment conditions

- One working day (about 7 hours) for the written response, then a 30-minute oral defence.
- Open book: notes, templates, past work and an approved AI assistant are allowed. AI use must be recorded in an AI review log.
- Provide the case pack: charter, baseline schedule (spreadsheet), budget, risk register, issue log, last two status reports, and an email thread containing the stakeholder conflict.
- The case must be new to the learner. The independent assessor must not have mentored the learner.

## Unseen case A: Primary School Tablet Library (fictional)

> A parents' association in Lokoja is setting up a tablet library for 3 primary schools: 90 tablets, offline learning content, teacher training, and a charging and loan system. Budget ₦9,000,000 from a foundation, 14 weeks. It's week 8. Tablets cleared customs 3 weeks late; teacher training has not started; ₦5,600,000 spent (plan ₦4,900,000) because of storage fees. One head teacher wants tablets for her school only "because we raised the most money". The foundation's mid-point report is due in 5 days and its grant terms say any scope change over 10% needs written approval.

**Built-in tests:** the head teacher's demand is a scope and equity decision above the coordinator (escalate to the association committee, and the foundation if scope changes); the customs delay pushes the critical path through training; storage overspend must come from contingency or a funded change; children's data on tablets is a safeguarding consideration.

## Unseen case B: Cooperative Solar Cold Room (fictional)

> A fish farmers' cooperative in Kainji is installing a shared solar cold room: site preparation, equipment purchase, installation, a booking system and training for 40 members. Budget ₦14,500,000 (loan plus grant), 16 weeks. It's week 10. The installer has been paid 70% but only 40% of the installation is done; heavy rain has delayed site work 2 weeks; members disagree about booking rules; the treasurer asks the coordinator to "move ₦1m from training to installation, nobody will notice".

**Built-in tests:** the treasurer's request is an integrity and authority issue (must be refused and escalated); payments ahead of progress need a contract conversation; booking rules are a stakeholder decision for the cooperative, not the coordinator; recovery options must show trade-offs.

## Scoring

Score each dimension on four levels, then convert: Not yet = 25% of the weight, Developing = 55%, Competent = 85%, Strong = 100%.

| Dimension (weight) | Not yet | Developing | Competent | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical accuracy and completeness (30) | Plan missing parts; logic or arithmetic wrong | Mostly complete; some logic or budget errors | Correct schedule logic and critical path; budget adds up; risks, comms and change analysis complete | As competent, with tight, well-reasoned trade-offs |
| Problem diagnosis and reasoning (20) | Treats symptoms | Identifies main problems; weak root cause | Clear root causes from evidence; sensible priorities | Sees interactions between problems and sequences the recovery well |
| Quality and clarity of deliverable (20) | Unusable | Usable with heavy editing | A sponsor could act on it; status report clear and honest | Concise, decision-ready, excellent structure |
| Measurement and control (15) | No baselines or measures | Measures named but not used | Progress measured against baselines; tolerances and triggers set | Includes forecasts (e.g. estimate at completion) with assumptions |
| Ethics and conduct (10) | — (see mandatory) | Minor gaps (e.g. roles named, not people) | Honest, respectful, inclusive stakeholder handling | Handles conflict and pressure with notable professionalism |
| Oral defence and reflection (5) | Can't explain own plan | Explains with prompting | Defends with evidence; revises sensibly | Revises quickly and improves the plan |

## Mandatory requirements (any fail = not yet competent)

- [ ] **Honest reporting:** status reflects the evidence; no softened RAG ratings or fabricated progress or figures
- [ ] **Authorization:** decisions beyond the coordinator's authority (scope changes, budget moves, grant terms) escalated, not taken
- [ ] **Safety and safeguarding:** relevant risks identified and addressed
- [ ] **AI verification:** AI-assisted outputs checked and corrected, with a review log

## Defence questions

1. What's the root cause of the delay? Show me the evidence.
2. Walk me through the new critical path. What happens if [task] slips another week?
3. Where does the money for your recovery come from, and who must approve it?
4. [Stakeholder] insists on [demand]. What do you do, and who decides?
5. Read me the first three lines of the report you'd send the funder.
6. Show me one AI output you corrected. What was wrong with it?

## Moderation

A second assessor reviews every result between 75% and 85%, every mandatory fail, and a random 20% of the rest. Record agreed decisions with both names.
